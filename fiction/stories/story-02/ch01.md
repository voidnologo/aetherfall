# Chapter 1

Thessaly Vane was early.

That was the first wrong thing. Covenant representatives didn't arrive early to meetings in taverns. They arrived precisely when they intended to, usually after the other party had waited long enough to feel the imbalance. Mira had seen Thessaly do it twice now: the measured entrance, the unhurried survey of the room, and the way she settled into a chair as though she'd chosen it from a catalogue of better options and found it merely adequate.

Tonight she was already seated when Mira came through the door. Corner table, back to the wall, a cup of tea untouched in front of her. Her coat was road-dusted. Her hair, usually pinned with architectural precision, had a strand loose at the temple.

Thessaly Vane had hurried. That was the second wrong thing.

"Mira." A nod, not a greeting. "Sit."

Mira sat. She caught Hesper's eye at the bar and got a small shake of the head: *she's been here twenty minutes, ordered tea, hasn't touched it, hasn't talked to anyone.* Hesper Brack read people as most people read broadsheets, and her assessments were free if you knew how to ask.

"I have a job," Thessaly said. "Time-sensitive. I need your Society mobilized by morning."

"Good evening to you too." Mira unbuttoned her jacket and leaned back, letting the chair take her weight. The Wet Ember was warm tonight. The coal fire was doing its work, and the gas lamps threw steady amber light across the worn wood of the bar. Two factory workers near the door were arguing about last season's boxing. A Greycoat inspector she didn't recognize nursed a whiskey alone. It was the usual crowd, minus anyone who mattered. "Morning is soon. What's the rush?"

Thessaly folded her hands on the table. Her rings caught the firelight: silver Covenant sigils, jewellery that was decorative in the same sense a uniform is. "Six days ago a Sootborn trader came to the northeast market to buy supplies. Rope, lanterns, preservation jars. Field equipment. She was overheard discussing what she called 'the site' with a merchant she apparently trusted." Thessaly's mouth thinned. "The merchant's discretion was less reliable than the trader assumed."

"Overheard by whom?"

"By enough people that the information reached us through three independent channels within forty-eight hours."

Mira did the arithmetic. If the rumour had reached the Covenant through three sources, it had reached other ears through others. It was already wide. "What's the site?"

"A structure in the Wilds, northeast of the city, near a Sootborn settlement on the forest edge. The trader described it as a pre-Tear industrial site the Wilds swallowed when the zone manifested. An old mill complex, from her account, or something like one. But she said the inside no longer matches the shell. The Aether has remade the interior into something the original builders wouldn't recognize. The Sootborn explorers who found it couldn't get all the way in, but they saw enough to report something significant inside." Thessaly paused. "A container. The descriptions are vague and secondhand, but the Covenant's assessment is that this warrants immediate investigation."

"Vague and secondhand," Mira repeated. "You're sending us into the Wilds on marketplace gossip."

"I'm sending you into the Wilds on the best intelligence available, which is imperfect. The Covenant is aware of the limitations." Thessaly's composure didn't crack, but something shifted behind her eyes, a flicker of the urgency she was working to contain. "We are not the only party who received this information. I have reason to believe the Ashworth Collective is acting on it too. If they reach the site first and bring the artifact under the Collective's control, the Covenant loses access permanently."

So it was a race, and the research expedition was a polite name for it.

Mira studied Thessaly's face in the firelight. The Covenant of Embers leaned towards Awakening. They believed the Aether mattered, that it was revelation rather than disease. An artifact from a pre-Tear structure was exactly what fed their worldview: evidence that the forces at work in the world predated the catastrophe everyone blamed them for. Proof, maybe, that the Veil had been thinning for longer than anyone admitted.

That was worth hurrying for. It was worth sending a Society with exactly one completed job into territory none of them had ever worked.

"Why us?" Mira asked. She wasn't fishing for compliments. She was doing the arithmetic again.

Thessaly had the grace not to dress it up. "You can leave immediately. Your Society has no prior obligations that would delay deployment. And the Covenant's other assets are committed to work that cannot be interrupted at short notice."

*You're available and expendable.* Mira heard the translation clearly enough and didn't take it personally. It was honest, and honest was more useful than flattering.

"The pay?"

Thessaly named a figure. It was good, better than the Harker job, with a bonus for confirmed recovery. It would keep the Ledger comfortable for two months, three if Mira was careful.

"We'll need supplies. Field gear for Wild Zone work. Provisions for... how far is this settlement?"

"Two days' travel on the northeast road, based on the trader's route."

"Two days there. Unknown time in the Wilds. Two days back. Call it a week, assuming nothing goes wrong." Mira almost smiled; things always went wrong. "We can be on the road by first light."

Thessaly nodded once. From her coat she produced a sealed envelope: the Covenant's formal commission, signed and stamped, authorizing Ash & Veil Recovery to act on its behalf. The wax was still warm. She'd prepared it before coming.

"One more thing," Thessaly said as Mira took the envelope. "The Sootborn settlement is not a Covenant interest. The people there have no obligation to assist you, and the Covenant makes no claim on their cooperation. How you handle that relationship is your professional judgment."

Which meant: *we know they won't help you, and we're not going to pretend otherwise.*

Mira pocketed the commission. "I'll send word when we're back."

"I'll be waiting." Thessaly stood, collected her coat and left without touching her tea. Hesper watched her go with the expression she reserved for people who ordered drinks they didn't intend to drink.

---

The flat on Harrier Street was two rooms above a cobbler's shop, reached by an outside staircase that groaned in weather and creaked in calm. The Society had been there three months. It smelled of leather and lamp oil, and of the faint copper sweetness that drifted east from the Quarter on warm evenings.

Kael was cleaning his revolver at the kitchen table when Mira came in. Aldric was reading in the corner chair, a book balanced on his knee and a cup of tea actually being drunk, which put him one up on Thessaly. Sera sat cross-legged on the floor by the window with her back against the wall, writing in the journal she never let anyone else touch.

Three faces looked up. Mira hung her jacket on the hook by the door.

"We have a job," she said. "First light. Pack for a week in the field."

Kael set down the cleaning rod. "Where?"

"Northeast. Past the farmland, into the Wild edges. There's a Sootborn settlement near the tree line, two days on the road. Beyond it there's something in the Wilds that the Covenant wants found."

"Something," Aldric said. He closed his book, keeping his place with one finger. "That's specific."

"A structure. Pre-Tear, apparently. The Sootborn found it and word got out. The Covenant wants us to locate it and recover an artifact from inside." Mira pulled a chair from the table and sat. Now that she'd stopped moving, the day's tension settled into her shoulders. "The intelligence is thin. Marketplace rumours, traced back to a Sootborn trader who talked too freely. But the Covenant is treating it as credible."

Sera looked up from her journal. "The Wilds. The actual Wilds?"

"The edges. Near the settlement, not the deep forest."

"That's still..." Sera stopped herself, but her eyes were bright, and it wasn't fear. It was something hungrier. "I've never been in a real Wild Zone. Not a strong one."

"You'll get your chance." Mira looked at Kael. "The Covenant thinks the Ashworth Collective heard the same rumours. If they've sent Ironwork, they're either ahead of us or right behind. Speed matters."

Kael's expression didn't change, but his hand came to rest on the revolver he'd been cleaning. It was a reflex rather than a decision. "Rules of engagement?"

"This is a recovery job, not a fight. We find the site, assess the artifact, bring back what we can and report what we can't." Mira held his gaze. "But we'll be outside city limits, with no Greycoat presence, up against a rival Society backed by the Collective. So pack accordingly."

Aldric had taken off his spectacles and was polishing them, as he did when he was thinking something through. "A pre-Tear mill," he said quietly. "In the Wilds. That part fits. The Wilds were settled land before the Tear: farms, villages, processing plants for the grain that came off them. When the zone manifested, anything standing there was swallowed. Most of it rotted or collapsed or was pulled apart by the growth. A few durable structures stayed upright, in outline if nothing else." He frowned. "But if the trader's right that the interior's been remade and the original purpose isn't legible inside, that's something else. That's the Aether doing more than preserving. That's active reconstruction. I've read speculation about it. I've never read a confirmed case."

"The Sootborn explorers got partway inside," Mira said. "They found something they couldn't retrieve and came back for supplies. That's when the information leaked."

"What kind of artifact?" Aldric asked.

"A container. That's all we have."

Aldric put his spectacles back on. "I'll need the full kit. Measuring instruments, materials for documentation. If this is genuinely pre-Tear, the site itself may matter as much as whatever's inside it."

Mira nodded and looked round the room. Kael was already working out logistics. Aldric was already planning his research. Sera already felt the pull of the Aether waiting at the end of the road. Three people she'd chosen, doing exactly what she'd chosen them for.

She didn't tell them the rest: the expendability, the sense that the Covenant was throwing them at a long shot because failure would cost only a Society nobody would miss. That calculation was hers to carry. She carried it as she carried everything, quietly and without asking for help.

"First light," she said. "Travel light, move fast, and don't talk about where we're going until we're past the city limits. I don't want to find out who else is listening."

The flat settled into the focused quiet of preparation. Kael reassembled his revolver with the ease of a man who could do it blindfold. Aldric opened his satchel and laid his instruments on the table in rows for inspection. Sera closed her journal and went to the window. She looked east towards the Quarter, where the air was thick and sweet and the shadows moved on their own.

Somewhere out there, past the farms and the coaching inns and the last ward-posted village, the Wilds waited. In the Wilds, inside the shell of a building where people had once worked, something that shouldn't exist sat behind defences nobody understood. It held answers to questions nobody had yet thought to ask.

Mira took the Ledger from the shelf and noted the commission. The numbers were good. For now, that was enough.
