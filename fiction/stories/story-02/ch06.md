# Chapter 6

The inside of the structure was worse than the outside, because there was no outside left in it.

From the clearing the mill had at least been a mill: the shape of a grain elevator, the footprint of a loading dock, the bones of a place where people had once worked their shifts, gone home and come back to do it again. Over the threshold, the mill ended. The corridor that should have been the receiving hall wasn't one. There was no chute manifold where the chutes should have been, no catwalks overhead, no footings for the roller stands, no bolt holes in the floor where machinery had stood for decades. None of the scars a working building carries. Sera glanced back at the daylight in the opening they'd come through, the one fixed reference that still made sense, framed by what was left of the loading bay. She found it comforting for all of six seconds. Then the corridor ahead curved, slightly, in a way she hadn't noticed it curving, and she put the comfort away for later.

The corridors were corridors, the walls were walls and the floor was a floor. But the proportions were made for something not quite human, and the angles came from a geometry that didn't care about the one Sera had grown up in.

She went first. Nobody had asked her to. The Aether inside the structure was so dense and strange that she was the only one who could see.

Not literally; there was light. A pale glow came from the walls themselves, from veins of something running through the seamless material like capillaries in skin. It pulsed slowly and irregularly, in a rhythm close enough to a heartbeat that her own pulse tried to match it. In that light the corridors ran in directions the eye could follow but the mind had trouble accepting. Left became forward became down, by degrees, as a river turns, so gradually that you didn't notice until you were facing somewhere you hadn't meant to.

"Sera." Kael's voice behind her, steady. She was grateful for that. "How are you doing?"

"The Aether is thick in here. But it's different from the forest. In the forest it felt alive. In here it feels..." She reached for the word. "Stored. Like it's been collected and kept. Like a library, but for power instead of books."

"Can you cast?"

She held out her hand and pulled a thread of force. It came too easily, rushing to her fingertips so eagerly that she flinched. A ball of pale fire blossomed in her palm, blue-white and brighter than she'd intended. The shadows it cast moved independently of the light, sliding along the walls in patterns that were almost recognizable, almost meaningful.

"Yes," she said. "I can cast."

The interior was a labyrinth, but not a random one. There was a logic to the corridors. Sera could feel it as she felt the Aetheric current, a pattern just below the threshold of understanding. Halls branched and rejoined. Rooms opened off the main corridors, empty or nearly so. Some held smooth, featureless pedestals, empty, though they looked made for display. Some had panels set into the walls, of a different, darker material, warm to the touch and etched with lines that might have been writing or circuitry or something else entirely.

"Don't touch those," Aldric said behind her. His voice was tight with the strain of a man equally terrified and fascinated. He had his notebook open and was sketching as he walked, in short, precise strokes. "The etched panels. I have no frame of reference. They could be inert. They could be active components. Until I can study them properly..."

"Aldric." Mira, behind him. "Keep up."

They pressed deeper. Kael marked each turn with his knife, a cross scored deep into the pale material of the left-hand wall. It resisted more than wood or stone and took real force to mark. But the scoring stayed. In a place that offered few anchors, that small fact was one.

At the fourth turn, the wall moved.

Sera saw it happen. She was watching Kael with half her attention, as she watched every professional in her life, while the other half worked. His knife was in the wall, drawing the second stroke of the cross, when the surface *shifted*. It didn't lurch. It settled, like a sleeper turning over. Suddenly the wall was three inches closer to Kael's hand, and his blade skipped off the new surface and cut across the meat of his forearm.

He made a small sound, not a word. It was the sound of a man who had told his body not to react and found his body ignoring him. The blade went back into its sheath in one movement. He pressed his other hand flat over the cut and stepped back from the wall.

"Kael." Mira was at his shoulder before anyone else moved.

"Clean slice. It's fine."

It wasn't fine. The cut was perhaps four inches long, running diagonally across the outside of his forearm, and blood was already darkening his sleeve. Sera could see the edge of it. The blade had been sharp enough to open the flesh cleanly, without ragged edges. It would close well, if it closed. In here, Sera wasn't sure anything would do what it should.

"Aldric," Mira said.

Aldric was already shrugging his satchel off one shoulder, his scholarly panic set aside for the other thing he was good at. He took Kael's arm, examined the cut with the detachment of a man who did this for a living, and pressed his palm flat above it.

"Hold still."

The Mend went in *too* easily. Sera watched Aldric flinch and adjust mid-cast, his jaw tightening as he pulled the spell back from whatever size it had wanted to be. The wound knit from the ends inward. It didn't close fully, but enough. The blood stopped spreading. The raw pink line left behind was still a wound, but one that wouldn't reopen if he was careful with it.

Aldric let the spell go and breathed out unsteadily. "That wanted to be bigger than you needed. I held it."

"Thanks."

"Bandage it anyway. The seal isn't as clean as I'd make it in a dead zone."

Kael nodded once. Mira was already pulling a strip of gauze from his belt kit. She wrapped the forearm while Kael held it out. Neither of them spoke; they had patched each other up before, and the work went fast. When she finished, the bandage was neat and snug and didn't restrict his wrist.

Kael drew his knife again and marked the next wall with the same confident stroke, standing a little further back this time. The cross scored cleanly. The wall didn't move.

"Walls are learning," Kael said.

"Walls are moving," Sera said. "Whether they're learning is a different question."

The first shift came ten minutes in. Or five, or twenty; in here Sera's sense of time was even less reliable than in the forest.

The corridor they were walking through changed. It wasn't dramatic. The walls didn't move and the floor didn't tilt. But the proportions shifted. The ceiling was higher than a moment ago and the corridor narrower. The left turn Sera had felt in the Aetheric pattern was now a T-junction, offering left and right.

"That wasn't there," Kael said.

"No," Sera agreed.

They looked at each other. Kael wore the face he always wore when a situation was bad enough to be simple: no uncertainty, no second-guessing, only a flat decision about whether to go forward or back. He looked at the junction, then behind them at the corridor they'd come through, still there and still marked. Then he looked at Sera.

"Which way?"

She closed her eyes. The current inside the structure was complex, layered and braided, flowing several ways at once like a river with different currents at different depths. But under the complexity there was a pull, faint and constant, the gravitational weight of something dense. The artifact.

"Left," she said.

They went left.

The deeper they went, the more the structure changed round them. The shifts were subtle. A room was larger when they looked back at it than when they'd passed through. A straight corridor curved. A doorway didn't exist until Sera looked directly at it, and then had always been there, as if the structure responded to being observed.

The technology in the walls grew denser. The glowing veins were thicker and brighter here, and the panels came more often, larger and more complex, their etched lines forming patterns Aldric couldn't stop staring at and couldn't begin to decode. Something behind the walls hummed. This wasn't the Galvanic hum of the factory districts. It was at a different frequency, felt in the teeth and the breastbone, where the mechanical and the magical met.

"Both," Aldric breathed. He had stopped, his hand pressed flat against a panel, caught between wonder and distress. "The resonance in these walls. It's both. Aetheric and Galvanic. Not competing. Not cancelling each other out. Working together. That shouldn't... the literature says..."

"Aldric," Kael said.

"This changes everything. If these fields can coexist, if they were *designed* to coexist..."

"*Aldric.* Hand off the wall."

Aldric snatched his hand back and stared at his palm. There was nothing to see. But Sera had seen the flash, a ripple in the Aether round his hand as he touched the panel, as though the structure had tasted him and found the data interesting.

"Don't touch the walls," Kael said. It wasn't a suggestion.

They pushed on. The pull grew stronger. Sera was navigating almost entirely by feel now. The visible layout of the corridors couldn't be trusted, but the current didn't lie, and the dense point she was following was getting closer. She felt it as a stone in her chest, warm and heavy, drawing her forward like iron to a lodestone.

The chamber appeared at the end of a corridor shorter than it should have been. One moment they were walking down a hall stretching into glowing distance. The next, the hall ended at a wide arched opening with edges smooth as poured glass, and beyond it was the room.

The chamber was circular. Its ceiling rose higher than any they'd passed, into darkness Sera's pale fire couldn't reach. Panels covered the walls, every surface inscribed with the etched lines. The glowing veins converged here, running in from the corridors like streams feeding a lake, and their concentrated light filled the room with a cold blue-white radiance.

In the middle, on a pedestal of the same seamless material, sat the container.

When Sera saw it she felt something she couldn't name. It wasn't recognition or familiarity. It was a resonance, as if a tuning fork in her chest had been struck by the sight of it. The container was small, perhaps two feet long, a foot wide and half a foot deep. Its surface was smooth and dark, the colour of deep water, and it took in the chamber's light without reflecting it. It looked solid. It looked *sealed*, in a way that went beyond a closed lid, as a word in a language you don't speak is sealed: present but out of reach.

"There," she said. Her voice sounded strange in the chamber, too clear and too close, as if the room had been made for speech and was surprised to hear some at last.

The others filed in behind her. Aldric made a sound that wasn't a word. Kael was silent, his eyes sweeping the chamber for threats. Mira stood at the threshold a long moment before stepping through. Sera watched her take the measure of the room and the odds, as she always did, and come up with a number she didn't like.

"Can you reach it?" Mira asked.

Sera walked towards the pedestal. The density of the Aether in the chamber was extraordinary. The air itself was visible, thick with glowing particles, and every breath tasted of copper and of something older that had been sealed in this room longer than she could guess. She reached for the container.

Her hand went out towards it. She felt her arm extend and her fingers close on air. But when she looked, she was standing at the entrance of the chamber again, six paces back, facing the pedestal, exactly where she had started.

"What..."

She tried again. This time she watched her own feet and counted her steps towards the pedestal. One. Two. Three. Four. She looked up. She was at the entrance, six paces back.

"Sera?" Kael's voice was careful.

"I can't reach it. The room puts me back."

Aldric tried next. After three steps he was at the entrance again, blinking, his hand still reaching for a pedestal ten feet away that might as well have been ten miles. He tried a Reveal. It tore off his fingers with the same hot eagerness as everything here, and narrowing it in this saturation left him grey-faced and breathing unevenly. What it showed him made him sit down heavily on the floor.

"The space between us and the pedestal isn't real space," he said thinly. "Or it's real space that's been folded. Walking through it returns you to where you started. It isn't a barrier. It's a loop."

Kael tried the direct approach. He walked at the pedestal with the stride of a man who didn't believe in impossible rooms, and found himself back at the entrance with the rest of them, knife still in hand. His expression suggested the room had just joined a very short list of things that had managed to surprise him.

"It's not keeping us out," Sera said. She could feel that the defence, if it was one, wasn't hostile. It wasn't even active in any way she could identify. The space was simply *arranged* so that approaching was impossible, like a staircase that only went up whichever way you climbed. "It's just made that way, like a lock. It's not fighting us. We just don't have the key."

Aldric was staring at the container from across the room, the blue-white light in his spectacles. "Look at it," he said. "The material, the precision. That isn't Aetheric. The zone, the forest, the spatial distortion, that's all Veil. But the container looks *manufactured*. Machined. It has the qualities of Galvanic engineering, but there's no Engine signature. Or there is, but it's..." He trailed off, his researcher's vocabulary failing before something his categories couldn't hold. "It shouldn't be here. Not in this shell, not at the centre of whatever the interior has become. Whatever it is, the people who built the mill didn't leave it. It was placed here. Or it was made in this room, by whatever's been rebuilding the inside."

The chamber's light pulsed once, slowly, like a breath.

"We need to go," Kael said. He wasn't urgent. He spoke with the measured certainty of a man who knew when a situation had given him everything it was going to give.

Sera looked at the container once more. The struck tuning fork in her chest was still vibrating with something that wasn't sound. She didn't understand it, and she wanted to. She wanted to stand in this room until understanding came, until the lock opened and the sealed thing showed her what it held.

But Kael was right. They'd found it and confirmed it, and they didn't have the key.

She turned and followed the others back into the corridors. Behind her the chamber pulsed, patient and sealed and very, very old.
