# Chapter 8

The nosebleed stopped after ten minutes. Aldric knew because he timed it by Kael's watch, the only measure he trusted, though he didn't trust it much.

He sat on the warm ground of the clearing with his back against a tree at its edge, his notebook open on his knees, and wrote. The pencil was unsteady in his fingers. The Anchor had cost him more than he'd estimated, and mending Kael's cut inside the structure had taken what was left. His reserves weren't empty, but they were low enough that another significant casting would put him somewhere he didn't want to be.

He wrote anyway. The details had to be caught now, while they were fresh and the adrenaline kept his observations sharp. Later his memory would soften the edges and tame what he'd seen into something his training could accommodate. He didn't want that. He wanted the raw data, however uncomfortable.

*Shell: pre-Tear processing mill, identifiable by silhouette and footprint. Grain elevator, roller mill, loading dock. Structurally intact in outline — the Wilds have not collapsed it. Confirmed pre-Tear origin per local testimony (T. at settlement well). Estimated abandoned when the Wilds manifested, ~50 years ago. Exterior material appears remade — concrete and timber replaced in-place by a pale seamless material, original form preserved. A cicatrix rather than a ruin. The mill is the scar the mill became.*

*Interior: bears no relation to the shell. No machinery foundations, no catwalks, no floor-fixture scars, no chute trenches. None of the signatures of a working mill. The interior has been hollowed and remade by something that did not consult the original function. Corridors follow non-standard geometry. Angles internally consistent but do not correspond to Euclidean expectations. The space shifts — slow reconfiguration of layout, as if the interior's floor plan is mutable. Not random: the shifts follow patterns I cannot yet identify.*

*Material (interior): seamless, pale, warm to touch, accepts scoring (knife marks) but resists, luminous veins running through it. Not stone, not metal, not ceramic. Not visibly related to the mill's original building materials. The Aether has not warped the mill — it has replaced it from the inside out.*

*The chamber: circular, high-ceilinged, convergence point for luminous veins. Spatial defense — approach to the central pedestal results in return to point of origin. Not a barrier. A loop. The space between the threshold and the pedestal is folded. No hostile intent detectable.*

He paused, looked across the clearing at the structure, and looked back at his notebook.

*The container.*

He'd seen it for perhaps ninety seconds, from thirty feet, through air thick with Aetheric light, under extreme stress. Ninety seconds was not enough. But Aldric had spent twelve years training his observation, and the training didn't switch off because he was frightened.

*Container: approximately 60cm x 30cm x 15cm. Dark material — absorbs light rather than reflecting. Surface appears smooth and featureless at distance. Seated on a pedestal of the same material as the structure itself.*

*Critical observation: the container's visual qualities are inconsistent with its environment. The remade interior is luminous, organic in its geometry, saturated with Aetheric resonance. The container is dark, precise, angular. Its proportions suggest manufacture — the kind of exactness that comes from machining, not growth or casting. If I did not know where I was, I would identify it as a Galvanic artifact. The precision, the surface finish, the proportions — these are the qualities of engineered objects.*

*But there is no Engine signature. No hum. No resonance of the kind associated with Galvanic technology. And yet the container sits, unmistakably, at the center of an interior remade by Aetheric force — an interior in which Aetheric and Galvanic qualities are interwoven in the wall material itself.*

*The mill is pre-Tear. The shell confirms that. Pell's grandfather ran it.*

*The interior is not pre-Tear. The interior is whatever has been building itself inside the shell in the decades since the Wilds came in. The container, then, is not necessarily pre-Tear either. It may have been placed, or grown, or assembled in the chamber by whatever agency is remaking the building from within. The timeline is intact. What I cannot yet answer is: what is the agency. What is it building. What is it building toward. And why, after fifty years of work, has it sealed this object behind a lock that nothing at our disposal can reach.*

He stared at the page. Then he closed the notebook and put the pencil in his coat pocket.

He would need to think about this, though not now. Now his head ached, his nose had only just stopped bleeding, and they still had to get back through the Wilds to the settlement. But eventually, in a quiet room with his references and his cross-indexed notes, he would have to sit with the implications of what he'd seen and follow them wherever they led.

He was not looking forward to it.

"Aldric." Mira crouched beside him. "How are you?"

"Depleted. Not incapacitated." He assessed himself with the clinical honesty his training demanded. "I can walk. I can cast minor workings: Detect, possibly Reveal, at reduced range and higher cost. Mend is available, but I'd rather not unless someone is actively dying. Shield is possible once, maybe, and then I'm done for the day."

"Honest. Thank you." Mira's hand rested briefly on his shoulder. It was a rare gesture from her, and he knew it was genuine rather than for show. "Can you do a Reveal? Wide sweep. I want to know if we're alone out here."

Aldric considered the cost. A Reveal in this saturation would be expensive, not because the Aether would resist but because it wouldn't. The spell would leap off his fingers like a dog that had waited too long for its walk. The work would be in dragging it back and narrowing it, refusing to let it swallow every detail of the forest for a mile round and drown him in returns. But he knew what Mira was thinking about. Kael had been watching for it since they left Ashwick: the tyre tracks on the road, the rival Society somewhere behind them. They were in the Wilds with confirmation of something extraordinary, and no guarantee that the people chasing the same prize would let that confirmation reach the Covenant.

He cast Reveal.

To the others it was invisible. To Aldric it was like throwing a stone into still water and watching the ripples not only travel but speed up. The Aether didn't resist; it *leapt*. The spell went wider and faster than he'd fed it, reaching further in its first second than he'd tried to reach at all. The returns came back at once, a flood that crested against the inside of his skull and tried to dump itself there in one unsorted heap. He clamped down. Mid-cast, he narrowed the spell's mouth as a glassblower narrows a neck, forcing the range back into usefulness and letting the excess bleed off into the saturated air. Every yard of width he shed cost him a throb behind the eyes like an arriving thunderstorm.

The clearing was clear. So was the forest immediately round them. The structure behind them was a blind spot; the Aether inside it was so dense the Reveal slid off like water off glass.

But to the west, towards the settlement and the path they'd taken in, the Reveal found people. Four, maybe five. The Aetheric interference muddled the returns, shapes blurring in and out of definition, but they were there. The spell couldn't see weapons, but they moved in the tight, purposeful formation of people who expected to use them. They were between the clearing and the settlement, moving east.

Towards them.

The headache crested. Aldric let the spell collapse and pressed his hand to his temple. His vision swam. He breathed through it as he'd been taught: three slow breaths, let the cost settle, don't fight it.

"We're not alone," he said, more thinly than he meant to. "Four or five people, armed, moving east through the Wilds between us and the settlement. Moving in formation. Not casual."

Kael's knife was already in his hand. "Distance?"

"Hard to say, with the spatial distortion. Maybe a mile. Maybe less. The Reveal wanted to keep going and I had to pull it back. My returns are clear to about that distance, and then they're lost in what I was bleeding off."

"Ironwork," Mira said.

"The Collective sent them to find the same site. They've been ahead of us or behind us since the road out of Ashwick. Tyre tracks past the waypoint, then pushing further than the village man thought wise. They found the settlement, found our trail and followed us in." Aldric wiped his nose. There was no blood this time, but the reflex was there. "And they know we found it. If we get back to Ashwick, the Covenant knows. The site's confirmed, and the Collective loses the advantage of secrecy."

The implication sat in the clearing like a fifth person. Out here in the Wilds, beyond any authority's reach and any witness's eyes, a rival Society could do whatever it judged necessary to stop that confirmation reaching the city. And the Collective had invested enough in secrecy to justify a great deal.

"We can't go back the way we came," Kael said, reading the tree line with eighteen years' practice at sizing up threats. "They'll be on our path. We need another route."

"There may not be one," Aldric said. "Sera navigated by the current, and the current flows in particular directions. Finding a different path through the distortion without her original route..."

"I can do it." Sera was on her feet, eyes bright, hands steady. Of all of them she was the least depleted; the saturation that drained the rest of them was feeding her. "The current has several channels. I followed the strongest one in. I can find a different one going out. It'll be harder. Longer, maybe. But I can do it."

Mira looked at Kael. Kael looked at the tree line.

"Then we go now," he said. "Different route. As fast as the forest lets us."

They gathered their gear. Aldric shouldered his satchel and felt the weight of the notebook against his ribs. It held his observations, his sketches, and questions he didn't yet have the tools to answer: evidence that someone wanted to keep from ever reaching the city.

He was not a fighter. He was a scholar who'd ended up in the field because his funding was cut and a woman he respected had offered him a job that used his skills. He carried a walking stick, not a weapon, and the most dangerous thing about him was information.

Right now, that was dangerous enough.

"Stay close to me," Sera said. She was looking into the forest with her head tilted, reading the invisible currents. "The route I'm finding goes north first, then curves back west. It's longer. But it avoids the direct line."

"Do it," Mira said.

They left the clearing. The structure watched them go with its glowing, patient indifference. Somewhere in the forest between them and safety, armed people were walking towards the place they'd just left, and the Wilds stretched in every direction like a puzzle that kept rearranging its pieces.

Aldric walked, and wrote nothing, and carried everything.
