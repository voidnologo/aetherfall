# Chapter 9

They heard Ironwork before they saw them.

There were voices in the forest, muffled by the dense foliage and distorted by the Aether's games with sound, but unmistakably human. Commands in a clipped cadence. Boots crunching through undergrowth. A metallic sound Kael identified without thinking: a rifle bolt cycling.

They'd been moving for what Kael estimated was an hour, though the estimate was worthless. Sera had led them north from the clearing along an Aetheric current that wound through the forest like a river across a floodplain, neither straight nor fast, but navigable. The trees were thick enough to hide them and the undergrowth dense enough to muffle their passing. But the Wilds' spatial distortion worked against secrecy as it worked against navigation. A sound that should have been half a mile behind them was suddenly close, then far, then close again, and its direction kept shifting.

"Stop." Kael held up a fist. The others froze.

He listened. The voices were to the south, between them and the direct route back to the settlement. Three, maybe four. They moved with the discipline of trained operators, but they were frustrated. The forest was fighting them as it had fought the Society, and their formation was ragged.

"They're on our original path," Sera whispered. "Following the current I used coming in. They must have found Kael's knife marks on the trees."

"Can they track us on this route?"

"Not by the marks. But if they have anyone with Aetheric sensitivity, they could feel the current I'm following now."

Kael weighed it. Ironwork Solutions leaned Galvanic. It was the Collective's preferred Society, equipped with Engine-enhanced gear and trained for zone operations. They might not have a caster. But "might not" was not odds Kael would bet four lives on.

"Keep moving. Faster."

They moved. The forest shifted round them in the slow, dreaming rearrangement that had become familiar enough to navigate but not to trust. A ridge appeared on their left that hadn't been there minutes ago. A shallow, cold stream crossed their path, its water clear as glass and faintly metallic. They crossed it and kept going.

The first shot came from the south.

It wasn't aimed at them. It was fired into the forest, at a target the shooter couldn't see, in frustration or as a signal. It sounded wrong: a dull, stuttering bark instead of the clean crack of a rifle in open air, a firearm fighting its own mechanism in the saturation. The bullet went somewhere, probably not where it was aimed.

The second shot was closer.

Kael drew the revolver and checked it. The action was still sluggish; the cylinder turned grudgingly, like a wheel in mud. He cocked it by hand, feeling the mechanism resist. The bandage Mira had wrapped round his forearm tugged when his wrist flexed, and he adjusted his grip without thinking about it. In these conditions the revolver was strictly single-action: cock, aim, fire, cock again. No rapid follow-up. Every shot was a commitment.

"Kael." Mira was beside him, arc pistol in hand. The Galvanic weapon was worse off than his revolver. The crystal chamber that powered it was dim and the hum of its charge barely audible. "The arc pistol is marginal. How's the revolver?"

"One shot at a time. The action's too slow for anything else."

"I've got the derringer." She pulled the holdout from her boot, a two-shot mechanical pistol simple enough to work in heavy saturation. "Two rounds. Make them count."

"There are four of them."

"Then we'd better not miss."

A shout came from the south, disturbingly close. The Wilds had compressed the space until Ironwork's voices sounded at talking distance, though they might still be a hundred yards off. Or fifty. Or twenty. The forest guaranteed nothing, including how much ground lay between you and the people trying to kill you.

"I can push them back," Sera said.

Kael looked at her. Her eyes were too bright, the blue-white ring round her irises visible even in the forest's dim green light. The Aether here was feeding her, filling her reserves while it drained everyone else's. She looked alive in a way that was beautiful and wrong, a flame burning too hot.

"Do it controlled," Kael said. "Nothing you can't rein in."

"I'll try."

"Don't try. Do."

Her jaw tightened. She nodded.

They heard them first. Then they saw them.

Ironwork Solutions came through the undergrowth in a loose file: four operators in reinforced coats, carrying rifles and sidearms with the easy competence of people who used them regularly. Their gear was good and their formation professional. Their faces were set with the grim focus of people who had a job to do and wouldn't be talked out of it.

The lead operator saw Kael at the same moment Kael saw him. There was a beat, less than a second between recognition and action, and then everything happened at once.

Kael fired. The revolver barked once, flat and muffled in the saturated air. The shot was true; eighteen years of marksmanship didn't evaporate because the mechanism was reluctant. The lead operator staggered, hit in the shoulder, and his rifle dropped from nerveless fingers.

Sera threw force.

She didn't shout or gesture dramatically. She held out one hand, palm forward, and the air between her and the Ironwork line burst. It wasn't an explosion so much as a concussion, a wave of raw force that tore through the undergrowth and hit the three standing operators like a wall of moving air. They went down, thrown backward, their formation shattered. One hit a tree trunk with a sound Kael heard through the distortion with sickening clarity.

She'd thrown more than she meant to. Kael saw it in her face, a flash of something other than triumph. Where the wave had passed, the undergrowth was flattened in a cone, ferns bent to the ground, branches snapped, the air shimmering with leftover energy. One of the operators wasn't moving. The others were struggling to rise, dazed and bleeding.

There was no time to assess. "Move!"

They ran. Sera led, navigating by instinct and Aetheric sense, ducking through gaps in the undergrowth that seemed to open in front of her and close behind. Kael ran rearguard, revolver cocked, knife in his off hand. Behind them the shouting rose as Ironwork recovered and regrouped, one voice above the others ordering pursuit.

A shot cracked through the trees. It sounded wrong again, muffled and distorted, but the bullet was real enough. It hit a tree six inches from Aldric's head and sprayed bark chips into the air. Aldric flinched and didn't stop.

The forest shifted.

The ridge that had been on their left was suddenly ahead of them, cutting across their path like a wall. Sera veered right without breaking stride, following a current Kael couldn't feel. The trees thickened and the light dimmed. Behind them the shouting grew distant, then suddenly close, then distant again. The Wilds were folding the space between pursuer and pursued, and the result was chaos. Neither side could hold a steady distance. The forest was shuffling them like cards.

"Shield!" Aldric's voice was strained. The spell went up behind them, an invisible wall that resisted like heavy water. It wouldn't stop a bullet, but it would slow a man. Aldric stumbled with the cost, and Mira caught his arm and kept him moving.

Another shot went far wide, its path bent by the saturation. The Aether was doing to firearms what it had done in every Wild Zone in the world for fifty years, making them unreliable. Making them liars.

But they were still firing. One shot in five might fly true, and one true shot was all it took.

Kael stopped.

Someone had to, and stopping was what he'd been trained to do: turn and face what was behind him and buy the people ahead enough time to get clear. He put his back to a trunk wide enough to cover his silhouette, raised the revolver and cocked it.

An Ironwork operator came running through the ferns, rifle up, twenty yards out and closing. The rifle was jamming. Kael could see him working the bolt frantically, trying to force a round into a chamber that didn't want it.

Kael shot him in the leg.

The revolver coughed. The operator went down clutching his thigh, his rifle clattering into the undergrowth. Behind him a second operator appeared, saw Kael's position, and fired.

The shot hit the tree. Bark exploded beside Kael's ear. He ducked, cocked, leaned out and fired again, and missed wide. The Aether pulled at the bullet as it pulled at everything mechanical. It wasn't much, not enough to matter at close range, but at twenty yards through saturated air the drift was real.

The operator fired again. His rifle jammed.

Kael didn't wait. He backed away at first, keeping the tree between them, then turned and sprinted north after the sounds of the others crashing through the undergrowth. Behind him the operator swore, a viciously human sound in a forest full of things that weren't.

The forest helped.

It wasn't deliberate, and it wasn't kind. But the spatial distortion that had tormented them all day chose this moment to fold the space behind Kael like a curtain drawn across a stage. The crashing and shouting and failed shots grew distant in a way that had nothing to do with running speed. The ridge reappeared on their left, then behind them. Suddenly the undergrowth thinned and the trees opened, and the air smelled different: still Aetheric, still saturated, but lighter and less dense. The edge of the deep zone.

Sera was ahead of him, pulling Aldric by the sleeve. Mira was beside them with the derringer still in her hand, both shots unfired. Kael caught up and fell into step.

"Everyone whole?"

"Whole enough," Mira said tightly. There was blood on Aldric's temple from the bark fragments. It wasn't a wound, but it looked alarming. Sera's eyes were glowing. This was no longer the faint ring from before. Iris and white were both lit from within, a full blue-white. She didn't seem to notice, or she noticed and chose not to address it.

"Did you..." Sera began, looking at Kael, at the blood on his knife, at the revolver with three rounds left.

"They'll live," Kael said. He didn't know whether that was true. The leg shot had been clean, but the operator Sera had thrown into the tree had been very still. "We need to move."

They moved. The forest lightened round them by degrees. The cathedral spaces gave way to something more ordinary, the canopy thinned and the undergrowth lost its aggressive density. The glow faded. The copper taste in the air weakened. Kael's revolver loosened in his hand as the action smoothed and the mechanism remembered what it was for.

They were coming out of the Wilds.

The settlement appeared through the trees as the light turned from green-gold to grey, the natural grey of an overcast sky in the ordinary world. There was the clearing, the buildings, the impossibly lush gardens, and a thin line of chimney smoke rising from the largest house.

Kael counted heads once more. Four, all standing, all moving under their own power, which was more than he'd expected an hour ago.

Sera's eyes were still glowing.

He decided not to mention it yet. There would be time. Right now the priority was distance between them and whatever was left of Ironwork Solutions in the forest behind.

They walked into the settlement. The Sootborn watched them come, and nobody needed to ask what had happened in the Wilds.
