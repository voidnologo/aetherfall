# Chapter 3

The Wilds tasted like the Quarter on its best days, multiplied by ten, and then by something that didn't have a number.

Sera had grown up with the Aether as background noise. It was the faint copper sweetness that drifted east from the Veilwright Quarter when the wind was right, the prickle on her skin when she passed near the zone boundary on an errand for her mother. She'd cast in moderate Aetheric areas, where the power thickened round her hands like warm water and she could pull threads of force from air rich with something she couldn't name but could always feel.

This was different. The forest beyond the tree line hummed, lower and older than the mechanical hum of the Galvanic quarter. It was how the earth might sound if you could press your ear to the bedrock and hear the world breathing. The air was heavy with it. Every breath tasted of copper and green growing things, and of something mineral and sweet underneath both, like rain on hot stone. The compass on her cord had stopped pretending to work. Its needle turned in slow circles, pointing nowhere and everywhere, as if confused by too much direction.

They reached the settlement within an hour of entering the forest edge. It sat in a clearing where the canopy thinned enough to let in the grey afternoon light. Someone had worked here with purpose. Trees had been felled and their stumps smoothed flat, some for tables or seats. Four buildings of salvaged timber and local stone stood in a rough circle, their roofs thatched with something like dried fern but thicker than any fern Sera had seen. Gardens flanked the nearest building, indecent with growth. Tomato vines hung heavy with fruit that shouldn't exist in early spring. Herbs stood waist-high. A row of something flowered in deep violet, each bloom the size of Sera's palm.

"Aetheric cultivation," Aldric murmured beside her. "The zone promotes growth. If you know what to plant and how to tend it, you can produce all year round."

"It's beautiful," Sera said.

"It's also evidence of sustained moderate-to-strong Aetheric saturation. The growth patterns are consistent with..."

"It's *beautiful*, Aldric."

He adjusted his spectacles and didn't argue.

A woman met them at the edge of the clearing. She was perhaps forty, lean and brown-skinned, her hair tied back with a strip of cloth. Her hands were dirty from the garden. Her expression was clean: she'd seen them coming and had already decided how this would go.

"You're from the city," the woman said.

"From Ashwick." Mira stepped forward, easy and open, the version of herself she used with people who had reason to distrust strangers. "My name is Mira Cade. We're Ash & Veil Recovery, an Adventuring Society."

"I know what a Society is." The woman's gaze moved across the four of them, pausing on Kael's revolver and Aldric's satchel before it settled on Sera. Something in her face shifted. She recognized, not Sera herself, but what Sera was: the sensitivity, the way she stood with her face slightly upturned, breathing the thick air like someone who'd finally surfaced after a long time underwater. "And I know why you're here. Lenna talked."

"We're not here to take anything from your community," Mira said.

"No. You're here to take what's beyond it." The woman folded her arms. "I'm Pell. I speak for this settlement, inasmuch as anyone does. You're welcome to buy water and food. You're welcome to rest. You are not welcome to stay, and we won't guide you into the Wilds."

"We haven't asked for a guide."

"You were about to." Pell's mouth thinned. "Everyone asks. Everyone comes here with their commission papers and their patron money and assumes that because we found it, we owe them a path to it. We don't. We built this place for ourselves, not as a waystation for every faction in Ashwick to use when something shiny turns up in the forest."

Sera could feel Mira's diplomacy pressing against Pell's wall, and knew which would give first. Mira was adaptable. Pell was planted. You didn't uproot a woman like that with charm or money. She had come to the edge of the Wilds on purpose and decided what she would and wouldn't give long before anyone from the city arrived to test it.

They bought food: dried fruit, bread dense with seeds, and strips of smoked meat at a price that expressed Pell's contempt for city economics. The settlement's other residents watched from doorways and garden rows. A man splitting wood stopped and leaned on his axe. Two children sat on a smoothed stump and stared with the unblinking focus children have before they learn to pretend they aren't looking. An older man by the well raised a hand to Mira. It was hard to tell whether it was a greeting or a warning.

While the others haggled over provisions, Sera drifted to the settlement's eastern edge. She couldn't help it. The Aether pulled at her as gravity pulls at water, and the strongest current ran east, deeper into the forest. From the corner of the last building she could see where the clearing ended and the Wilds resumed. The trees here were enormous, their trunks too wide for two people to reach round, their bark silvered and smooth. Their branches began thirty feet up and spread into a canopy that filtered the light into something green and luminous. Between the trunks the undergrowth was thick with ferns and moss, and with something that glowed faintly even in daylight. Its pale blue-white light pulsed so slowly you could mistake it for a trick of the eye.

It was terrifying. It was the most beautiful thing she'd ever seen.

She stood there until her hands stopped shaking. It wasn't the cold. It was the effort of not reaching for the power that filled the air like heat from an open furnace. It would be so easy here. In the moderate zones of Ashwick she had to search for threads of force, thin and elusive. Here they were thick as rope, winding through the air and the ground and the trees. If she reached out they would come to her eagerly, and give her more than she could hold.

That was the part that frightened her.

"It's a lot, isn't it?"

Sera startled. The older man from the well had come up beside her without her noticing, which showed how completely the Aether had taken her attention. He was perhaps sixty and weathered, with the deep-set eyes of someone who'd spent years squinting at things that weren't entirely visible.

"You can feel it," he said.

"Yes."

"How long?"

"Since I was a child. It's never been... it's never been like this."

The old man nodded. He looked east into the forest with equal parts affection and caution, as a fisherman looks at the sea. "We came here because it feels like home. Most of us, anyway, the ones with the sense for it. The city was too thin. Too much hum and metal, and the sweetness all scraped away by the factories. Here the air is right." He paused. "The ones who went deeper said it gets more than right. They said it gets loud."

"Loud?"

"Their word, not mine. I didn't go with them." He scratched his jaw. "They were gone two days, they said. Might have been more. Might have been less. Time's funny past the second ridge. You'll find that out, if you go in."

He looked at her again with the same recognition Pell had shown, and something else as well. Sympathy, maybe, or concern.

"The young woman who went the farthest has your look. The way you stand in the air. She came back quieter than she left. Not hurt. Just listening to something the rest of us couldn't hear."

He walked away without offering more. Sera watched him go, then turned back to the forest. The glow in the undergrowth pulsed, slow as a sleeping heartbeat. The trees swayed in a wind she couldn't feel on her skin.

Somewhere in there, past the second ridge, past the point where time stopped being reliable, something waited inside a building the Wilds had taken half a century ago and never stopped working on. She was going to find it. The knowledge sat in her chest like a coal, warm and certain.

She went back to the others. Mira was talking with Kael in low tones. Aldric was sketching in his notebook from memory: a child's drawing he'd noticed on the wall of one of the buildings, all angular lines that looked like architecture.

"They won't guide us," Mira said as Sera came up.

"I know." Sera looked east. "I can feel the way."

Mira studied her a moment with the look that weighed what you were offering against what it would cost. Then she nodded.

"We leave at dawn."
