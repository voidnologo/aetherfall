# Chapter 7

The structure shifted while they were leaving.

Kael felt it before he saw it, a deep, slow vibration in the floor like a ship settling in its berth. Then the corridor stretched. It wasn't fast or violent. The walls didn't lurch or crack. They moved like a sleeper shifting under a blanket, the hallway lengthening by inches over seconds, until the turn ahead, the one he'd marked with a cross on the left wall, was twice as far away as before.

"Move," he said.

They moved. Kael took point, then Sera, then Aldric, with Mira at the rear. Fighters in front, then the navigator, then the healer and observer, and at the back the one who made decisions about what happened behind them. They'd learned the order in the foundry tunnels on their first job. It had worked then because it was simple and used everyone's strengths.

The structure didn't care about their strengths.

The first knife mark was where it should have been, a cross on the left wall at the T-junction where Sera had guided them left. They turned right, retracing their path. The second mark was there too, twenty paces on, where the corridor had curved. The third mark was on the wrong wall.

Kael stopped. He'd scored every mark on the left wall at waist height, deep enough to gouge the pale material. This cross was on the right wall at shoulder height, in the same material, at the same depth. It was his mark, in the wrong place.

"The walls swapped," Sera said tightly. She was reading the Aether in the corridor as you'd read smoke to find a fire, trying to tell what had moved and what was still true. "The corridor's mirrored. Left became right."

"Can you still feel the way out?"

She closed her eyes. The pale fire she carried guttered, then strengthened, and the shadows on the walls shifted in patterns Kael deliberately didn't try to read. "Yes. The outside is that way." She pointed down the corridor. "The Aether flows towards open air. I can follow it."

"Then forget the marks. Follow the current."

They moved faster. The structure went on shifting round them in a slow, geological rearrangement. It wasn't hostile and it wasn't helpful: a building with the metabolism of a living creature and the indifference of stone. Corridors they'd passed through changed. One widened, another narrowed. A ceiling dropped a foot. A floor developed a slope that hadn't existed ten minutes earlier. The glowing veins in the walls pulsed faster, as if the structure's heart had quickened.

Kael navigated by Sera's direction and his own instincts. Eighteen years of walking streets had given him an inner compass that didn't depend on magnetic north, a sense of distance covered and direction turned that worked without the tools now failing round him. In his Greycoat days he could find his way through the warren of streets behind the Ashworth Foundry in the dark, blindfold, from memory and a count of his steps. This was harder, because the space itself was lying to him. But the lie wasn't perfect, and Kael had spent his career catching lies.

"Left here," Sera said.

They turned left into a corridor that hadn't been there on the way in. It was broader than the others, with a higher ceiling and thicker, brighter veins in its walls. Under the Aether's copper sweetness there was a faint mechanical smell, the ghost of ozone. Engine resonance. It wasn't strong enough to suppress the zone, but it was there, like a bass note under a melody.

"This is new," Aldric said. He was stumbling slightly. The earlier Reveal had cost him, and the shifting didn't help his footing. But behind his spectacles his eyes were alert, still cataloguing. "This corridor wasn't part of the layout we came through. The structure opened a new path."

"Opened, or shifted into place?"

"I don't know. Both? Neither? The distinction assumes the structure follows rules I understand, and it doesn't."

The new corridor led to a junction larger than any space they'd passed through, almost an atrium, where six corridors met. The ceiling rose into darkness. The veins from all six converged here, their light pooling into a brightness that made Kael's eyes ache.

Sera stopped at the edge. "Something's wrong."

Kael scanned the space: six corridors, six possible approaches, six possible exits. The atrium was empty. The floor was the same warm, seamless material. The light was intense but steady. Nothing moved.

"Wrong how?"

"The current." Sera's hands were open at her sides, fingers spread, head tilted in the listening posture that meant she was reading the Aether deeply. "It's circling. The flow in this room goes round, like a whirlpool. If we step into it, I'm not sure which corridor we'll come out of."

"Which one leads out?"

She pointed to the third corridor from the left, its opening a little wider than the others. "That one. I think. The outside air is that way. But the current between here and there is..." She made a swirling motion with her hand. "Scrambled."

Kael looked at the atrium, then at the corridor she'd indicated. Thirty feet of open floor lay between, through a current that might send them anywhere.

"Aldric. Can you do anything about the current?"

Aldric leaned against the corridor wall, pale and breathing hard. The evening's casting showed in the grey of his skin and the tremor in his hands. "I could try an Anchor. It's a Warding spell. It stabilizes the local field and creates a still point. But in this saturation, the cost would be..."

"Can you do it or can't you?"

Aldric straightened. He took off his spectacles, polished them and put them back on: his habit, his reset. When he spoke, his voice was steady. "I can hold it for about thirty seconds. Long enough for all of us to cross if we move quickly."

"Do it."

Aldric stepped to the edge of the atrium and took a breath, then another. Then he cast.

Kael saw it take hold. The Anchor settled over the atrium like a hand pressed flat on churning water. The circling current shuddered, resisted, and then stilled. The air went flat. The veins dimmed, as if the structure itself was startled by the sudden calm.

Aldric made a sound through his teeth, the sound of a man absorbing a cost he'd agreed to pay. His face went white. His hands locked at his sides.

"Go," he said.

Kael went first, across air that was still and dead and tasted of nothing. The Anchor had pushed the Aether back and made a pocket of something almost neutral in the heart of the saturated structure. The thirty feet took eight seconds. He reached the third corridor and turned.

"Clear. Move."

Mira grabbed Aldric's arm and pulled him forward. Sera followed, reaching back to steady him when he stumbled. They crossed in a tight group, Aldric's face rigid with concentration. The Anchor held, though the veins in the walls flickered uncertainly, as if the structure was pushing back against the imposed stillness.

They reached the corridor. Aldric dropped the Anchor.

The effect was immediate. Behind them the circling current roared back into the atrium, a warm, dense gust as the Aether rushed to fill the space Aldric's spell had emptied. The veins flared bright. The atrium hummed.

Aldric sat down hard on the corridor floor. A thin line of blood ran from his left nostril to his upper lip. He wiped it with the back of his hand and stared at it with the expression of a man who knew exactly what it had cost and was working out whether the ledger balanced.

"Aldric." Kael was already coming back for him.

"I'm fine." He wasn't. But he was conscious, and he moved when Kael pulled him to his feet. "The exit. Sera, the exit?"

"This way." Sera could feel it clearly now: the pull of open air, the current flowing out towards the forest and the world that made sense. "Close. Very close."

They ran. The corridor was shorter than it should have been, as if the structure was expelling them and the architecture squeezing the distance out. The pale light strengthened, then changed from the cold blue-white of the interior to something warmer and greener: forest light through an opening ahead.

The last hundred feet were the strangest. As they neared the outside, the walls lost their seamlessness in patches, and hints of what lay beneath showed through. Here was a stretch of pitted concrete, there a course of brick. A horizontal strip marked where a steel girder had once been set into the wall, only a ghost of it now. Either the shell was remembering itself or the interior was forgetting. They ran across the rectangle of ground where wagons had once backed up to the chutes. For a moment the floor became, impossibly, a concrete apron with worn drainage grates. Then they were out.

The opening where they'd entered was wide and smooth-edged. Rushing at it from the inside, Kael saw that it was framed by a mill's loading bay, its upper lintel still bearing the rust-streaked stub of an old hoist mount. Beyond it lay the clearing, and beyond the clearing the trees, the forest, the Wilds, the world.

They stumbled out into the clearing. The air hit Sera like a wall, warm and wet and saturated with the living forest's Aether. It was so different from the stored, contained power inside that she gasped.

Kael was counting heads before they'd all stopped moving. "Four. We're clear."

Aldric sat on the warm ground and put his head between his knees. Mira stood at the edge of the clearing looking back at the structure, her face unreadable. Sera turned to look too.

The mill sat in the clearing, pale and luminous and patient, still a mill in outline: the silos, the dock, the broken smokestack. The openings in its surface were darker now than before, as if the interior had dimmed after they left. The veins on the outside pulsed at the same slow rhythm. Slow as breathing. Slow as the shift bell that had once called men to work on this ground, on a schedule nobody alive remembered.

It had let them in and shown them what it held. Then it had rearranged itself round them and made them fight to leave.

It was neither hostile nor helpful. It was simply *there*, being what it was, following rules nobody alive could read.

Sera's hands were still trembling, and not from exhaustion. There was something she hadn't told the others. Inside the chamber, when the container pulsed and the resonance struck her chest, something had answered. Something in her, in the place where the Aether lived, had recognized the sealed thing and reached for it. It wasn't her will or her casting. It was something deeper and older, and entirely involuntary.

She hadn't reached the container. But something in her had wanted to, with a hunger that frightened her more than the shifting corridors, or the impossible geometry, or how nearly they'd been sealed inside the remade guts of a building that had long ago stopped being the mill its shell still remembered.

She kept that to herself. She was getting good at that.
