# Chapter 6

The vision was already dissolving.

Sera held on to it as you hold a dream after waking, cupping it in her mind and trying to see the details before they ran through her fingers like water. She had seen the tunnels as they would be rather than as they were: fully transformed, the ivory corridors extending and branching and joining, spreading under the city like roots under a forest. A network. The growth was directed. Each branch reached towards the others, and where they met they glowed with the same bone-bright threads that pulsed in the walls round her.

At the centre, or not quite the centre but the place all the branches reached towards, was something deep and vast and patient that she couldn't see clearly. It was a presence that wasn't a presence, a purpose she couldn't name. It had the quality Aldric had described in the mill, something made but not by hands. But it was vaster and older, working on a timescale that made human planning look like a child drawing in sand.

The details were going. She tried to hold the shape of it, the network, the convergence, the direction, but the particulars blurred even as she reached for them. What remained was an impression, a sense of scale, and the knowledge, certain as gravity, that what she'd seen was a trajectory rather than a possibility. The tunnels were growing towards something, and had been for a very long time.

She kept it to herself.

She didn't want to. The urge to share was strong, as it always was when something rearranged her understanding of the world. Aldric would want details, observations, the kind of precise description she wasn't sure she could give. Kael would want to know because knowing was how he protected people. Mira would want to know because information was how she protected everyone else.

But she couldn't describe it until she understood it herself, and she didn't. The network, the convergence, the presence at the centre: these were words she was pinning to sensations that didn't have words yet. If she tried to explain now, in the tunnels, shaking and depleted, she'd get it wrong. She would shrink something vast into something small, the small version would become the team's understanding, and the team's understanding wouldn't be enough.

She also didn't want to see Kael's face when she told him the tunnels were becoming something, and that she had looked into the becoming and it had looked back.

---

Something had changed, in her rather than the tunnels.

The moment she tore herself free of the artifact, the violence of the separation had left a mark. It wasn't physical. It had nothing to do with the exhaustion, or the glow in her eyes, or the trembling that was finally, slowly leaving her hands. It was in how she felt the magic.

Before the artifact, reaching for the Aether was instinct, natural as breathing. The river was always there, and she swam in it without thinking about the current, where it went, or what lived in its depths. She reached, it answered, and the conversation was simple and familiar and hers.

Now she thought about the current.

Now she knew the river went somewhere, that the somewhere was real, and that it had noticed her. The vast thing where the branches met had been neither hostile nor welcoming. It had been *aware*, in a way that was neither human nor inhuman but simply other. Its awareness had touched her as casually as she might brush a wall in passing. The touch had carried the vision, the vision had carried the scale, and the scale had shown her exactly how small she was beside what she was swimming in.

The river had a riptide. She had always known that in the abstract. Now she knew it as you know a stove is hot once you've touched it.

It didn't stop her casting. But now there was a beat between the impulse and the reach, a breath, a moment of conscious choice that hadn't existed when reaching was as automatic as blinking. She hated it and was grateful for it in equal measure.

---

The tunnels had shifted.

It was subtle, nothing like the mill's wholesale rearrangement, with corridors reshuffling as you walked them. But it was enough. A passage they'd come through was narrower, its walls perceptibly closer. A junction where they'd turned left now had four branches instead of three, the fourth opening onto a corridor of dense crystal that certainly hadn't existed an hour ago. Kael's knife marks were still on the walls, since the ivory substance held cuts. But some of the marks had moved, inches rather than feet. The surfaces they were carved into had shifted and carried them along, as driftwood carries carved initials.

"The route has changed," Kael said, flat and professional.

"It's growing," Sera said. "Still growing. It shifted while we were down here."

"Can you find the way?"

The beat. The breath. Then she reached, not for a spell or for power, but for the passive sense that let her feel the tunnels as you feel a draught: direction, distance, open and closed. Pathfinding didn't cost as casting did. She was grateful, because what she had left wouldn't stretch far.

"That way," she said, pointing at the passage they'd come through. "It's narrower now, but it goes."

It went for fifty yards. Then it didn't.

The passage they'd used to descend into the deep tunnels, the service access beneath the collapse zone, had narrowed further. The walls had grown inward and the pale material thickened, until the gap was something Kael could squeeze through sideways but Aldric, the tallest of them, could not.

Sera looked at the gap, then at her hands. She felt the fog: the heaviness in her reserves, the distance between her and the Aether, as if the river had stepped back and she had to lean further to reach it.

The beat. The breath.

She reached for Kinesis. The power came with the grudging cooperation of something tired of being asked. She shaped it against the wall and pushed, willing the gap wider. The ivory substance resisted for a moment, then gave slowly, like a door on heavy hinges. The gap widened enough for Aldric.

The cost landed at once. The hollow feeling deepened and her reserves dropped another notch. Her hands trembled worse than before the cast, the new fatigue piling onto what she'd carried since the barrier. She flexed her fingers and they answered slowly, as if the signal from her brain was travelling through something thick.

"Thank you," Aldric said as he passed through the gap. His eyes went to her hands and away. She knew what he was doing: counting, measuring, the silent arithmetic of a healer watching someone spend what they couldn't afford.

She appreciated the silence more than she would have appreciated concern.

---

They moved on. The upper tunnels were closer now. She could feel the Galvanic pressure reasserting itself above them as the staircase gradient stepped down. The crystals thinned and the glow dimmed. The world was remembering what it was supposed to be.

Then came another obstacle. The ceiling had lowered here, and crystal had grown across the path at head height in a lattice of pale material that blocked the passage like a frozen waterfall.

The beat. The breath. Longer this time.

She reached again and aimed Kinesis at the lattice, pushing it aside like a curtain. The power came, but she could feel the fog. The reach was longer and the answer slower, like shouting down a hallway that had grown since the last time. Some of the crystals shifted, but not enough. The gap was barely wide enough for Sera herself, let alone the others.

She had to cast again.

The second cast cost as much as the first for a smaller effect. The hollowing deepened. Her ears rang with the thin tone she'd carried since the barrier, fainter now, or perhaps she was too tired to hear it properly. The crystals shifted further and the gap widened. Kael went through first, then Mira, then Aldric. Sera went last. Getting through was a physical effort it shouldn't have been, and her arms shook as she pulled herself past the last crystal edge.

The returns were diminishing. Her body was telling her she was nearing a limit she couldn't see. She could feel it as you feel a cliff edge in the dark, by the quality of the air and the sound of empty space ahead.

Aldric looked at her after the second cast and didn't speak. His face said it for him: *that's enough.*

She nodded. It wasn't a concession to his authority; he had none over her casting, and they both knew it. It was an agreement between two people who understood the arithmetic. She was done. Whatever came next she would handle without magic, as Kael handled everything, with hands and will and a stubborn refusal to let the world set the terms.

---

At the junction between the deep tunnels and the upper drainage system, three people were waiting.

They weren't Fels. They were his crew, armed with a crossbow, a short sword and a truncheon. They were professional as Fels was professional: clean equipment, calm eyes, the patience of people paid to wait who didn't mind the work. The woman with the crossbow led them. She was short and broad-shouldered, her hair pulled back, her face as blank as people trained in violence learn to make their faces. She didn't introduce herself.

"The case," she said.

Mira stepped forward with the transport case. The latches were closed again and the Syndicate stamps visible. In place of the broken seals was a loop of twine tied in the knot a courier uses to pledge that the contents have been inspected and the case closed in good faith. The woman saw the twine. Her eyes flicked to Mira's face and back. She didn't ask about it, or about what they'd found in the tunnels, or about the state of the deep passages. Fels had told her what to collect and nothing more. That was how Fels operated: information was currency, and you didn't give it to employees.

"Follow us out," the woman said.

They followed, through the upper tunnels, the drainage system and the service passages, the familiar geography of infrastructure that still looked like what it was meant to be. The Syndicate crew moved fast and without talking along a route marked with chalk symbols Sera recognized from the way in. The escort was insurance rather than help. Fels trusted his crew to collect the case. He didn't trust the Society to deliver it unsupervised.

The storm drain. The night air on the foundry's west side. The smell of rust and old oil.

The Galvanic hum came back like a door slamming.

Sera flinched. She'd been dreading it for the last hundred yards, feeling the Aetheric warmth fade in steps as they climbed and the staircase gradient reversed. The change was as brutal as she'd feared. In three steps the Aether went from flood to drought. The copper taste vanished. Her compass stopped spinning and hung dead. The dry metallic pressure of the Factory Quarter settled over her like a weighted blanket of static. For hours she'd relied on that sense to guide them through shifting tunnels and growing walls, and having it smothered was physically painful.

Her eyes ached. Her ears rang. The thin tone spiked and then went silent, leaving only the ordinary silence of a city humming on frequencies she couldn't normally hear.

Depleted as she was, nothing was left to cushion the shift. She felt every degree of the smothering, and each one was a small, specific loss.

She bit the inside of her cheek and kept walking.

In the foundry yard, under the grey-orange light of the Factory Quarter's arc lamps, the Syndicate crew took the case from Kael's hands. The woman with the crossbow weighed it briefly and glanced at the twine knot on the latch without comment. Then she tucked the case under one arm and led her people into the shadow of the loading bay without a word.

The four of them stood in the yard. The arc lamps hummed. The cobbles were slick with condensation. Somewhere to the east, past the factory district and the gradient, the Veilwright Quarter breathed its copper air and moved its shadows, and Sera couldn't feel it from here. She was cut off, dry, empty in a way that sleep might fix but that tonight felt permanent.

As Mira watched the crew leave, her face showed the calculation for a moment before the mask came back: the mental arithmetic of what they'd given away against what they'd kept. They had handed over the case. They had kept the knowledge.

Sera's hands had stopped trembling. She didn't know if that was recovery, or whether she was simply too tired to shake.
