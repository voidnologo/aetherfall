# Chapter 5

The deep tunnels set Kael's teeth on edge.

It wasn't fear. He could work with fear. He'd been working with it since the first time he drew his revolver in a dark room and found that fear was just another input, information about the environment to process, use and set aside. Fear was functional. What the deep tunnels gave him was different: a wrongness that lived at the back of his skull, behind the part of his brain that tracked exits, counted threats and measured the distance between his hand and his knife.

The walls were close. The bone-pale substance, warm to the touch, fused at every joint, neither stone nor metal, pulsed with bright threading in a rhythm that almost matched his heartbeat. Almost. The near-match was worse than no rhythm at all. It was like walking beside someone whose footsteps nearly fell in time with yours, the almost-pattern dragging at something primal, as if the tunnel were trying to match him and not quite succeeding.

He checked his revolver.

It was automatic; a man who'd carried the same weapon for seven years thought no more about reaching for it than about blinking. But the action felt wrong. The cylinder didn't spin with its usual crisp authority. The mechanism was sluggish and reluctant, and the metal was cold in a way that had nothing to do with temperature. He'd cleaned and oiled it that morning, as he did before every job. It had been fine in the upper tunnels and fine in the service passages. Down here in the transformed deep it felt like a toy, a thing pretending to be a weapon.

He didn't holster it. But his grip shifted to the knife.

The knife always worked. It was steel, an edge, and the hand that held it, with no mechanism to misfire, no moving parts to resist, and no dependence on the forces fighting over the air round them. Kael had carried it since before his time with the Greycoats. It had never failed him in any zone or condition, in any situation where the world stopped following the rules he'd grown up with. The knife didn't care about the Aether or the Engine. It cared where you put it and how hard you pushed. Kael could respect that.

---

They found the cargo where Fels had said it would be, deeper than was comfortable. In this stretch of the transformed tunnel the bright threading in the walls converged into patterns that Aldric was sketching with an intensity close to compulsion.

The sealed transport case sat in a niche in the wall. Kael recognized the Syndicate stamps, the same industrial serial numbers he'd catalogued during the Harker investigation. It was standard smuggling equipment for moving valuable goods through tight spaces: reinforced composite, sealed joints, handles rated for rough transit. Professional.

The wall had partly grown round it.

Thin, glowing tendrils of crystal crept over the case, following its seams and edges like ivy up a fence. The case was being absorbed into the tunnel's new architecture as the old brickwork had been, and the process looked unhurried and inevitable.

The case was humming, lower and warmer than the Galvanic hum of the factory above. Kael felt it in his breastbone. The vibration came neither through the air nor through the ground but through some medium he had no word for. It kept time with the pulse in the walls. The case and the tunnel were talking to each other.

"It's been here three weeks," Kael said. "Whatever's inside is resonating with the environment."

"Correct." Aldric had stopped sketching. He was studying the case with the face he wore when professional fascination and professional concern were fighting for control of it. "The crystal growth has partly absorbed the container. I'll need to separate them."

Kael assessed the situation. Sera was hanging back, her face drawn with the fatigue she'd carried since the barrier. Her hands were still. Her eyes were their normal dark, the glow faded, but there was a watchfulness in them that hadn't been there in the upper tunnels. Aldric was already planning his approach to the case, his fingers making small preparatory movements. Mira was covering the corridor behind them, her attention split between the team and the empty passage. She was doing what she always did, watching everything so Kael could focus on the problem in front of him.

"Do it," Kael said to Aldric. "Carefully."

Aldric laid his hands on the crystal where it had grown over the case. Watching him from outside was different from watching Sera cast. With Sera there was always a moment of surrender, a visible instant when she opened herself to something larger and rode it. Aldric never surrendered. His jaw set. His breathing slowed. His hands moved against the crystal so deliberately that each millimetre seemed planned.

The crystal began to separate from the case, slowly, peeling back as bark peels from a log, clean and controlled. Kael could see the effort in a vein pulsing at Aldric's temple and the taut tendons of his forearms. The magic down here was pushing against whatever structure Aldric was imposing, wanting to amplify the spell and give him more than he'd asked for. Aldric held it back. Kael recognized the stance of a man holding something on a leash. The danger wasn't the enemy in front of you. It was the force behind you that wanted to help too much.

The crystal parted and the case came free. The wall settled afterwards with the same slow adjustment Kael remembered from the mill. The glowing veins near the case pulsed faster for a moment, then slowed to their normal rhythm. A system adjusting. A sleeper turning over.

Aldric stepped back. His hands were steady, but he was breathing harder than the physical effort warranted. He closed his eyes for a moment to reset. Kael noted it: the spell had cost Aldric something, and holding it to its intended size had cost him more on top.

Kael took the case. His hands were steadiest and his reflexes fastest. If what was inside was dangerous, he would deal with it before anyone else had to.

He broke the seals. The latches were stiff but worked; the crystal hadn't got inside. Within lay a smaller container, a sealed vessel of dark stone, palm-sized, resting in a foam cradle the Syndicate had fitted to hold it in transit. The stone was carved with symbols Kael didn't recognize, running in concentric rings round its surface. They pulsed with a faint light in time with the walls round them, stone and tunnel breathing together. The hum was louder here. It came from the vessel itself.

Aldric's intake of breath was audible. "That's not in the Covenant's catalogue. It's not in the Gradient Scholars' archive either." He leaned closer without touching it. "Lamplighter stockpile. They've been collecting artifacts, and nobody knew the scope."

"Fels knows the scope," Mira said from the corridor. "He had this. He was moving it through these tunnels for a reason."

Kael couldn't answer the question underneath that. Was the Syndicate moving the artifact through the tunnels for convenience, or for something else? The resonance between vessel and tunnel was no coincidence. The tunnel had grown round the case and the hum had synchronized. After three weeks of contact, carved stone and living corridor were already in conversation.

Something moved at the edge of his vision.

Sera was standing closer than she'd been a moment ago. Her compass was spinning fast, the brass clicking audibly against its housing. Her eyes had that look. It wasn't the backlash glow from the barrier but something quieter: fascination. It was how she had looked in the Wilds, when the forest opened into the mill clearing and she'd stood at the threshold of something impossible and leaned forward instead of back.

She didn't decide to reach for the artifact. That was what Kael saw: the absence of a decision. Her hand went out towards the carved stone, not deliberately as Aldric's spells were deliberate, but in response, as if something was pulling.

"Sera." His voice was sharp, one word meant to cut through whatever current was carrying her.

Her shoulders tensed. She heard him. He saw his voice reach her, wherever she was, and register as a warning she understood. But her hand didn't stop.

The magic happened before the touch. The air between her palm and the vessel thickened and shimmered, and the carved symbols brightened as she came near. Kael had no words for what he was seeing: some exchange between the thing in her and the thing in the stone, a call answered, a frequency matched. She wasn't trying to do it. The magic in her reached for the magic in the artifact as iron filings reach for a magnet. It wasn't a choice. It was physics.

Her fingers touched the outer casing.

Her eyes flared white.

It was the wrong colour, nothing like the blue-white that had been building since the Wilds. It was pure white, a brightness without colour. The dark of her irises was gone, and what replaced it looked less like anything biological than like a light source. She went rigid. Her jaw locked. Her body stopped being a body and became something receiving, a vessel something was pouring into.

Three seconds. Kael counted them, because counting was what he did when he couldn't act. One: her eyes blazing, the symbols on the stone blazing in sympathy, the veins in the walls pulsing in a sudden violent rhythm. Two: her hand pressed flat against the stone, trembling, her whole arm rigid with something that wasn't effort at all but its opposite, the strain of being opened wider than you'd chosen. Three: the hum in the tunnel reaching a pitch Kael felt in his fillings, in his ribs, in the knife handle against his palm.

Then she ripped herself free.

It was no gentle withdrawal. She tore her hand back as if pulling it out of wet clay, and the force of it sent her stumbling backward. Kael caught her, one hand closing on her shoulder, the other on his knife.

Sera's breath came in hitches. Her eyes were still wrong. The white was fading back through blue-white towards her normal dark, but slowly, like a lamp turned down in stages. She was shaking. This wasn't the casting tremor from the barrier. It was deeper, a full-body vibration coming from wherever the magic lived when she wasn't using it, as though the thing inside her had been struck like a bell and was still ringing.

She looked at him, and for a moment Kael saw something in her face he'd never seen there before. It wasn't fear. Sera was rarely afraid of the magic. She leaned into it. She rode the wave and trusted the current even when it carried her somewhere she hadn't meant to go.

This was different. It was the face of someone who had reached into the river and felt, for the first time, how deep it went: how strong the undertow, how far it could carry you if you let it, and how much it wanted to.

Caution: the first she had ever shown towards the thing that made her extraordinary.

"What did you see?" Aldric asked from somewhere behind Kael's shoulder. His voice was careful and clinical, a researcher asking a question he needed answered while the human part of him was visibly worried.

Sera's breathing steadied. She pulled herself upright and out of Kael's grip, slowly, as you stand up after a fall, testing that everything still works. "I don't know yet," she said.

It wasn't a lie. But it wasn't all of the truth, and Kael knew it as he knew most things about Sera, by watching and by the spaces in what she said.

"We move," Mira said. She'd appeared at Kael's side, taking in Sera's condition, Aldric's face and the vessel in its foam cradle, still pulsing faintly. "Now."

Kael closed the transport case and the latches clicked. The Syndicate seals he'd broken were a scatter of useless wax fragments now. He dug the spool of waxed twine out of his jacket and bound the latches with a courier's knot, the knot Greycoats used for chain of custody on evidence boxes. It wouldn't fool anyone, and it wasn't meant to. It would tell Fels the case had been opened and closed by people who wanted him to know they'd opened it. The hum went on, muffled but present, a voice talking through a closed door.

He shouldered the case and led them back the way they'd come.
