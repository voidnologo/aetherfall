# Chapter 2

The argument started before Mira finished the briefing.

"No." Kael said it flatly, as he said things that weren't negotiable. He stood by the window with his arms crossed, outlined against the grey morning light. He'd been standing since Mira began, which meant he had already decided and was only waiting his turn to say so. "We're not Syndicate contractors. We don't do jobs for people who open the conversation with blackmail."

"It's not that simple," Mira said.

"It is exactly that simple. This is the line I left the Greycoats to avoid crossing."

Aldric sat at the kitchen table with his Ashwick Job notebook open in front of him, its pages covered in his small, precise hand: measurements, observations, questions without answers. He'd been turning pages since Mira mentioned the tunnels. "The conditions Fels described," he said without looking up. "Syndicate runners are used to moving through moderate zone environments as part of their trade. If the Aetheric activity under the foundry has grown past what they can handle, that's a significant escalation from what we documented during the Harker investigation." He turned another page. "The crystalline formations. The ambient luminescence. My readings were anomalous but explicable within the existing framework. If the same formations have grown dense enough to close passages and create hostile conditions, we're looking at something comparable to the Wild edges." He paused. "I'd want to go down there whoever was asking."

Sera sat on the floor by the cold fireplace with her knees drawn up and her compass hanging from its cord round her neck. She hadn't spoken during Mira's account. She was looking at the compass, which was doing what it did when she was lost in thought, spinning in slow, lazy turns that had nothing to do with magnetic north.

"Sera," Mira said.

"I felt something down there." Sera's voice was quiet, not reluctant but sorting. "The first time, during the Harker job. In the tunnels. A resonance. I didn't understand it then, and I've been thinking about it for months and I still don't, but I want to go back." She looked up. "I can't tell you if that's curiosity or the Aether pulling. The difference might not matter."

Four positions, four different calculations. Mira weighed them as she weighed everything, by what the team could survive rather than by who was right.

"Here's what I know," she said. "Fels has information about our operations that he can sell to the Ashworth Collective, Ironwork, or anyone else who'd pay for leverage against a Society that's become more visible than we can afford. If we refuse, he sells. If we accept, we do a retrieval under duress for a criminal enterprise." She kept her voice level. The moral argument had no clean answer. What mattered was the operational reality. "Either way, we take damage. I'm choosing the damage I can manage."

Kael's jaw tightened. He didn't argue further, which was worse than arguing. It meant he'd accepted something he hated, and the acceptance would cost him in ways that wouldn't show until later.

Aldric closed his notebook. "What do we know about current conditions?"

"Fels's intelligence is three weeks old and comes from runners who couldn't handle what they found. The access point is the same storm drain we used on the Harker job, west side of the foundry, behind the industrial rubbish." Mira pulled a chair from the table and sat. "The foundry's still decommissioned, still in Galvanic territory, moderate zone. The tunnels under it showed early-stage Aetheric bleed last time we were there. If conditions have grown past what the Syndicate can manage, we should expect something like a moderate-to-strong Aetheric environment. Underground. Confined. No way to retreat if the environment turns against us."

Aldric took off his spectacles and polished them, as he did when thinking something through. "Then we should discuss resources." He put them back on and looked round the team like a doctor delivering a diagnosis. "I can sustain perhaps six or seven casts before I'm running on fumes. Healing is expensive. If someone is badly hurt, a proper Mend will take most of what I have left in one effort. Detection and shielding are cheaper, but they draw from the same well." He tapped the table once, as he did when he wanted a point to land. "I mention this so everyone understands that magic is a finite supply. I'll use it where it matters most, and I'll be judging what matters most as we go. Those judgments may not always match what feels urgent at the time."

"Sera," Mira said. "Your reserves?"

Sera tilted her head, considering. "Deeper than his. But less predictable. I burn hotter and I can't always choose how much I spend." That honesty was like her. Sera never pretended her path gave her control it didn't. "Between us we have enough for a careful expedition. Not a long one. Not a messy one."

"Then we don't make it messy," Mira said. "In, retrieve the case, out. Aldric documents what he can without stopping to study it. Kael marks our route. Sera navigates if conditions call for it. I'll handle the handoff when we surface."

She stood. The decision was made. What came next was the part she didn't share.

Mira had edited the briefing.

She hadn't done it dramatically, or exactly dishonestly. She'd given them the substance of Fels's proposition: the job, the blackmail, the carrot of intelligence on the Collective. But she'd softened the edges. In her telling the blackmail was a negotiation with teeth, a trade they had chosen because the alternatives were worse. She hadn't described Fels's tone, the pleasant, professional fury that made the offer feel less like business and more like a hand closing round their throats. She hadn't repeated his detailed inventory of what he knew, which proved his information was current and complete.

She had done it on purpose. The team would perform better believing they had chosen. Morale mattered, and it came from feeling you'd picked your situation rather than been cornered into it. If they thought Mira had negotiated from a position of limited options, they'd work with confidence. If they knew the Syndicate had them by the throat, they'd work with fear.

It was the right call. She was almost certain it was the right call.

Kael was watching her, though not obviously. He was checking his revolver, the habitual stripping and reassembly that was to Kael what polishing spectacles was to Aldric. But at each point where her account departed from what Fels had said at the Wet Ember, his eyes went to her face. He'd been there. He'd heard the original. He knew what she'd changed.

He didn't call it. His hands moved through the familiar motions, cylinder out, chambers checked, cylinder in, action smooth. His face gave nothing away except a faint tightness at the corners of his mouth, which Mira had learned to read as disagreement held in reserve.

She knew he'd noticed. He knew she knew. The silence between them held the shape of a conversation they weren't having, about whether managing your team's information was the same as lying to them.

"Gear check," Mira said. "Lanterns, rope, chalk for marking. Kael, your usual kit. Aldric, field notebook and medical supplies. Sera..."

"I know what I need," Sera said. She was already on her feet, the compass hanging still against her chest, pointing somewhere that wasn't quite north. Her eyes had the brightness they got when the Aether was calling. They weren't glowing yet, but they had the alertness of someone hearing a frequency nobody else could.

They were going back to the foundry and the tunnels, back underground where the rules were different and the walls were growing.

Mira opened the Ledger and looked at the numbers. The Covenant payment from the mill job was keeping them solvent for another two months, three if she was careful. Fels wasn't paying in marks. He was paying in information and the absence of catastrophe. That didn't balance a book, but it kept you alive to balance the next one.

She closed the Ledger, put it back on the shelf and picked up her coat.

The flat settled into the focused quiet of preparation. It was the same routine as before the mill expedition, the same economy of movement from four people who'd learned to pack for danger without discussing it. But the silence felt different. Before the mill they had been nervous and excited. Before the tunnels they were resigned.

Mira didn't let herself think about whether resignation was worse.
