# Chapter 4

The Syndicate had built a road down here.

Aldric recognized the signs from the Ashwick Job: painted markers at junctions, supply caches in waterproof boxes bolted to the walls, chalk symbols at the branches in a code he'd half decoded the first time. It was the infrastructure of a professional smuggling operation, kept up for years by people who used these tunnels as commuters use the tram. Some of the caches had been opened and emptied. Others were still sealed, abandoned when the route became impassable.

He paused at a junction where three tunnels branched, each marked in Syndicate chalk, each leading into deeper transformation. He needed to know what was ahead. The field notebook was open in his hand and the pencil was moving even as he decided.

He cast Detect.

He'd watched Sera cast the barrier and seen the current take her, the power flooding through her as something she rode rather than directed. Her magic was a river. Aldric's was a mechanism. There was no reaching in it. There was assembly.

The process was familiar and rehearsed, as close to automatic as something dangerous could get. He built a lattice in his mind, a framework of intention shaped by study, refined by practice and bounded by an exact understanding of what he wanted the spell to do. The lattice had dimensions: range, resolution, duration. He set them before he started, as an engineer sets tolerances before cutting metal. The Aether would fill the framework he provided, exactly as much as it could hold.

He felt the spell take shape. The power filled his structure as water fills a glass, pouring in from the edges, settling into the contours he'd drawn, finding the level he'd chosen. It was clean, ordered and predictable.

Except that down here, it wasn't quite.

The Aether pushed against his lattice, pressing at its boundaries. Far more power was available, and it wanted in. He could feel it as you feel a crowd pressing against a door: not hostile or aggressive, just present and insistent. He could have let it in, widened the lattice and cast a wider net. The information would be richer and the picture more complete.

He declined. He held the boundaries where he'd set them and let the spell resolve inside the frame he'd built. Control was the point. It was always the point.

The information arrived clean, organized and layered. The left-hand tunnel descended towards the collapse zone, its passage blocked by growth, as Fels had described. The tunnel ahead led to a Syndicate cache, recently disturbed. The tunnel to the right was new. It wasn't in his Ashwick Job notes. It was a branch that had grown since their last visit, with denser crystal along its length and a stronger Aetheric signature.

He paid the cost, and it was exactly what he'd expected: a specific, measured withdrawal from reserves he was tracking in the margin of his notebook. He made a small tick, as Kael counted ammunition. It was a professional account of a finite stock. The page already held a short row of ticks from the morning. The row wasn't long yet. It would grow.

"Left," he said. "The collapse is that way. The other routes lead to Syndicate storage and a branch that's new since our last visit."

"New branch?" Kael asked from the front.

"The tunnel system is extending itself." Aldric wrote it down. The pencil was steady and so were his hands. That was the difference between his path and Sera's: he knew what every cast would cost before he began. The price of that certainty was that he would never surprise himself.

---

He documented as they moved towards the collapse. The notebook gained new headings: Growth Rate, Pattern Distribution, Resonance Type. He was comparing what he saw now with notes from four months ago, and the comparison was doing something uncomfortable to his breathing.

During the Harker investigation the crystals had been scattered and irregular. It was early-stage Aetheric bleed of the kind the literature described in moderate zones, interesting but explicable. What he saw now differed in kind, not degree. The crystals followed the tunnel's geometry with deliberate precision. The bright channels in the walls pulsed in a coordinated rhythm, like a circulatory system. The ivory substance between the old bricks was warm to the touch and smooth as bone. Kneeling at a junction to look closely, he saw that it wasn't filling the gaps so much as replacing them. The original drainage works were dissolving into something new.

He'd seen this before.

In the mill.

The recognition hit him physically, though there was no gasp or stumble. It was a cold clarity behind the breastbone. The pencil went still while his mind rearranged itself. It was the same substance and the same warmth, the same quality of construction that looked made, but not by hands. The mill's interior had been completely transformed, its industrial bones entirely replaced. The tunnels were mid-process. He was watching the same transformation at an earlier stage, like a photograph of a painting before the artist finished.

The implications toppled one after another. If this was the same agency, it was working in two radically different environments. The mill sat in a strong Aetheric pocket of the Wilds, unusual but the kind of zone the literature could describe. The tunnels lay beneath a Galvanic factory, in a zone that should suppress Aetheric activity entirely. Whatever was doing this didn't obey the rules. It didn't care about the system the whole world had organized itself round for fifty years.

He wrote it down in three precise, understated lines. The notebook was not the place for the vertigo he felt.

---

At the collapse zone, Aldric cast Shield.

He made no announcement. He placed his hand on his chest and murmured the three words of structure, the verbal anchor for the lattice. It was a habit from training he'd never abandoned, though the words themselves were arbitrary. He felt the ward settle over him: a weight that wasn't weight, a presence on his skin neither warm nor cold. Protection, measured and allocated.

He'd chosen his scale before he started. This was the modest version of the spell. It couldn't turn a rifle round, but it would do for what a cave-in might send at him. The cost, duration and effect were all measured. He made the calculation automatically, before the words left his mouth, as he could look at a medicine bottle and know the dose. The tick in the notebook's margin would wait until he was outside the spell's window. Inside it, his attention belonged on the ward, not the accounts. They hadn't found the cargo yet, and the tunnels were getting stranger.

Ahead of him Sera moved carefully through the blue-white light. She was conserving herself. He could see it in the economy of her movements and in the way she kept her hands at her sides instead of touching the walls as she had in the upper tunnels. The barrier had cost her more than she'd admitted, and anyone who knew what to look for could see it: a slowness in her reactions, a dullness in eyes that normally caught everything, a faint tremor in her fingers when she adjusted the compass cord at her neck.

He thought about offering to teach her his methods, the lattice and the framework that turned chaos into something you could measure and manage. She'd listen politely and ignore every word, not from stubbornness but because her magic didn't work that way. It was the same force through a different kind of mind. She reached into the current and rode it. He built a vessel and let the current fill it. In the tunnels she'd raised a dome of force he couldn't have matched with twice the preparation and three times the reserves. She had also nearly collapsed afterwards.

He respected it, and he worried about it. Both feelings were real, and neither was useful at the moment, so he filed them with the observations and kept walking.

---

The collapse zone wasn't a collapse.

Aldric pressed his palm against the barrier and understood at once. The tunnel hadn't fallen. It had closed. The crystal formations had grown across the passage from both walls and met in a solid plug, warm to the touch, fused across its width and faintly threaded with the bright filament that ran through everything down here. It was architecture, not rubble. The tunnel had sealed itself as a wound heals, the two sides growing towards each other until they met.

"It's not damage," he said. "It's growth. The walls grew together."

Kael tested the barrier with his knife. The blade left a thin pale line in the crystal. The material took the cut without cracking or chipping. It was dense and integrated, not brittle.

"Can you get through it?" Mira asked.

"Not without reshaping it, and I'd rather save that." Aldric was already looking for alternatives. The Syndicate's chalk showed a junction fifty yards back, and below those marks were older ones. They weren't Syndicate. They were maintenance symbols from pre-Tear works older than the drainage system, marking service passages: narrower tunnels beneath the main channels, built for access to pipes and foundations nobody had serviced in fifty years.

They found the access, a corroded metal hatch in the tunnel floor whose hinges protested but worked. Below it ran a passage so tight they had to go single file, stooping, the ceiling brushing Aldric's head. It was dark; the glow hadn't reached this deep, and the walls were still honest brick and stone. The lanterns came back out.

The passages grew warmer. The air was close and smelled of something Aldric couldn't name. It lay beneath the familiar smells, older than the Aether's copper and rain or the dry metallic hum of Galvanic territory. It was the smell of a process that had run so long it had become the background.

They came out on the far side of the collapse into a tunnel that stopped Aldric in his tracks.

The brickwork was gone.

The walls were the same bone-pale substance he'd documented in the mill: fused, smooth, glowing faintly from within where the slow threadlight ran. Its pulse was deeper here than anything in the upper tunnels. There was no intermittent flicker, only a steady, sustained heartbeat that had been beating down here for years. Decades, maybe. The ceiling was smooth and faintly curved, the floor level and warm. The old drainage geometry was still legible in the proportions. This had been a tunnel once, before it became a corridor. But its substance had been entirely transformed.

It had happened long before anyone was watching, before the Syndicate ran smuggling routes through the upper levels, before the Greycoats chased Fen Harker into the foundations. All that time it had been going on in the dark and the deep, patient and steady and unknown, while the city above argued about Aether and Engine and which one was the threat.

Aldric opened his notebook to a clean page. His hand was not entirely steady. He titled the page in his small, precise hand:

*Deep Tunnels — Fully Transformed*

Then he began to write, because that was what he did when the world broke open and showed him something that didn't fit. He wrote it down and made it real on paper. And then he kept walking into the part that hadn't been written yet.
