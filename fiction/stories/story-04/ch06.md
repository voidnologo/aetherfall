# Chapter 6

*Moving.*

He was moving and he wasn't doing it. Something under him swayed side to side in a slow rocking, like a boat on a canal. Through the cloth under his back he could feel hands, not on him but on the things carrying him. He knew the hands, though he couldn't have said how. The left-hand front one was Mira. The back ones were Aldric and somebody smaller.

The smell of smoke was in everything: in the cloth, in his own hair, in the breath of the people carrying him.

He tried to say *put me down, I can walk*. Nothing happened. It occurred to him distantly that this was because he couldn't.

---

*Sky.*

Leaves moved slowly past overhead, green and gold and dark. He watched them go. Oak. Ash. Oak again. A beech with the light coming through so bright it hurt. More oak. He was scouting. Out of habit he was cataloguing the canopy, as he'd catalogued doorways and windows and the hands of men in taverns for twenty-three years. Good cover here. Bad sightlines. Someone could come down that slope and you'd never see them till they were on you.

*Stop it*, he told himself. *You're not working.*

He kept counting trees anyway. It was something to hold on to.

---

*Sera's face.*

It was very close and upside down; she must have been walking at his head. Her hair hung down either side of her face like a black curtain, jagged where she'd cut it herself with the kitchen scissors. Her eyes were wrong. They glowed a little, a pale, cold, steady light, frost with the moon behind it. There was dried blood under her nose.

"Stay with us," she was saying. "Kael. Stay with us. Please."

He wanted to tell her that her eyes were doing the thing again, and to wipe her nose. More than he had wanted anything for a long time, he wanted to say *I'm all right, kid*. His mouth wouldn't do it.

---

*Pain.*

It came and went. When it was coming it was very large and very far away, a storm he could see crossing a valley that belonged to some other place. Then it arrived, and it was his, and it was everywhere. It went on and on. Then it rolled off again and left him in the quiet after it, washed out, too tired to be afraid.

Something was wrapped round his chest. He couldn't see it, but he could feel a cool close pressure, a hand holding his ribs still. When the stretcher jolted on a root, the pain in his side flared but his ribs didn't move. He thought that was probably Aldric's doing. He should say thank you. He'd say it later.

---

*Water at his lips.*

It was cold and ran down his chin. He couldn't swallow. It hurt too much. His throat had forgotten how.

"Small sips." Aldric's voice, close and hoarse. "Come on. Try, Kael. Just a little. Try."

He tried. Some of it went down. Most of it didn't.

"Good. That's good. Again."

---

The day after his twenty-first birthday he'd shot a man. He'd been a Greycoat for two years. In a kitchen in the Low Docks the man had a knife and a sister he was trying to cut. Kael told him three times to drop it. The third time the man turned round with the knife up, and Kael shot him in the shoulder. He didn't die. Kael visited him in the infirmary afterwards and the man spat at him. That was fine; he'd deserved worse. Kael went home and was sick in the sink. His landlady heard and said nothing. In the morning there was a cup of tea outside his door.

He didn't know why he was thinking about that. It had been a long time.

He thought about his father, who'd laid track for the Northern line until the Wild took it. Sixty miles of steel and sleepers had gone under the trees in a single season. His father came home a quieter man. On Sunday nights he taught Kael to clean a rifle at the kitchen table, the parts laid out on a towel in the order they went back together. *Same order every time*, his father used to say. *Then your hands know it when your head's not there.*

His hands knew it. His head wasn't there.

He thought about a girl called Teodora, with freckles and a gap in her teeth, who sold flowers on the corner of Ludd Street the summer he was nineteen. He bought violets from her every day for a month and never once worked out what to do with them. He wondered where she was. He hoped she'd had a good life. He hoped somebody had bought her flowers who knew what to do with them.

He thought about Mira at a corner table in the Wet Ember last spring, her hands round a glass she wasn't drinking. *I'm putting together a Society. A charter, Kael. Our own. I need somebody I trust. I need you.* He'd been out of the grey coat a year by then, drinking slowly in a rented room. She'd known it and said nothing about it. She'd just asked.

*Yes*, he'd said. It was the easiest word he'd ever said.

He thought: *I don't want to die here.*

Then, slower, from somewhere further down: *If I have to, I'd rather it was here, with them, than anywhere else without them.*

He didn't say any of it. There was nobody to say it to who didn't already know.

---

*Stopping.*

Voices. Horses: he could smell them, warm and dusty and alive, and hear them shifting their feet. A clearing. Someone said a word he didn't know, in a Wild-edge accent, and someone else answered.

He was lifted and put down on something hard that smelled of straw and sacking. A cart. The Covenant's cart from the settlement; they must have brought it.

"How far?" Mira's voice, somewhere above and to the left. Very calm. Too calm.

"Eight miles." A man's voice, flat and Sootborn. "By the back trails. We wait for dark. They'll have people on the road by now."

"He might not have eight miles."

A pause. "Then we'd best go careful."

---

*Jolting.*

The cart had no springs. Every stone on the trail came up through the wood and the straw into his back like a fist. The cool hand round his ribs held, but everything else moved. Each bump found the hole in his side and opened it a little. The storm came over the hills again.

Somebody was holding his good hand, the right one. He thought it was Sera. He held on.

---

*Aldric, at dusk.*

His face was very close, lit from below by a little borrowed hand-lamp, so the shadows went up instead of down. He looked like a schoolboy telling a ghost story. He held Kael's eyelid open with his thumb and shone the light into it, then the other. He put two fingers on Kael's neck and held them there, his lips moving as he counted.

One lens of his spectacles was a spiderweb of cracks. Kael wondered when that had happened. He hadn't noticed.

"Still here," Aldric said, to himself more than to Kael. "Still here. Good man. We're close now. Stay with me."

He checked again an hour later, and an hour after that. Every time Kael surfaced there was Aldric's face, the little lamp, the fingers on his neck. Every time, *still here*.

---

*Firelight.*

The cart had stopped. Low voices, a lot of them. A dog barking. He was lifted again. This time it hurt so badly the world went white and then dark. When it came back he was lying on a real bed, on a rough wool blanket, with a fire burning in a stone hearth at the corner of his eye. The ceiling was planks. The room smelled of woodsmoke and boiled onions and lye soap.

The settlement. They'd made it.

Aldric's face again, different now. He'd washed it, or someone had made him. Without his spectacles his eyes looked naked and very tired, the skin under them purple. His jaw trembled.

"Kael." He waited until Kael's eyes found him. "Kael, listen. I'm going to do what I can now. I've rested a little and got a little back. It's going to go deep this time, and it's going to hurt. I'm sorry."

Kael couldn't answer. He moved his fingers on the blanket. Aldric saw it and nodded, as if it had been a whole sentence.

He put his hands on Kael's side.

The first one had been warm. This was hot. It went in under Aldric's palms and down and kept going, deeper than Kael had known there was to go, into places he had never felt and never wanted to. He felt it find the hole, and the other hole at the back. He felt it slide between them, through him, along the bullet's line, and *pull*. It wasn't pain, and it wasn't not pain. His whole body seemed to be arguing with itself, every part shouting at once. In the middle of the shouting something patient and stubborn kept saying *no, like this*, over and over, until the shouting stopped.

His ribs moved. He felt them go back, like a door closing.

The heat ran out of Aldric's hands. He took them away and sat down hard on a stool beside the bed.

Kael lay and breathed. In. Out. In.

It didn't bubble.

He was weaker than he'd ever been. His side still ached deep and dull and steady, like a bad tooth. His arm was splinted and strapped to his chest, throbbing. He felt emptied out and refilled with sand. But he could breathe. He could feel his own heart beating, slow and solid and his.

He opened his eyes properly. He hadn't, since the valley.

Aldric sat on the stool with his elbows on his knees and his head down. Sweat ran off the end of his nose. His hands hung between his knees. They weren't trembling; they were *shaking*, as hands shake in the cold.

"Aldric," Kael said. It came out as a rasp, a boot on gravel.

Aldric looked up.

"Thank you," Kael said.

Aldric blinked at him, and didn't seem to understand at first. Then the corner of his mouth moved in a small, contained smile, the smile of a man who didn't do it often and wasn't sure he was doing it right.

"Anytime," he said.

Kael breathed for a while. Then: "Next time. Your turn to get shot. I'll fix you."

Aldric laughed, short and dry and so tired it was almost a cough. "Deal," he said.

---

There were people in the doorway.

Mira leaned on the frame with her arms folded. She'd washed her face too, but she hadn't changed her clothes, and the blood on her sleeves wasn't hers. Her face was perfectly composed. Behind her and to one side stood Sera, a blanket round her shoulders like a cloak, holding on to the doorframe. Her eyes had stopped glowing. They were just eyes again, with the pale ring round them. They were wet.

"Hi," Kael said.

"Hi," said Sera. Her voice wobbled. She didn't wipe her eyes. She let them be wet.

Mira said nothing at all. She looked at him across the room for a long time, and he looked back. He could read her face as clearly as if she'd written it down: *I was scared. I am so glad you are here. I am not going to say that I was scared.*

He nodded to her. She nodded back. That was the whole conversation.

He closed his eyes.

---

He slept for three days.

He only knew it was three days because they told him afterwards. At the time there was only the sleep, deep and dark and warm, and sounds coming down through it from far away, as if heard from the bottom of a pool. A woman outside the window laughed at something somebody said. Children ran past shrieking, playing a game whose rules he couldn't follow. A well crank creaked and rattled. A dog barked. In the next room Mira and Aldric talked in low voices. He couldn't make out the words, but their shape was steady: two people planning something together. Overhead, soft and light, somebody's footsteps paced back and forth across the floor of the loft, as you pace when you're waiting for news.

Ordinary sounds. People living their lives. The world was going on around him without caring that he'd nearly left it, without needing him to do anything at all.

It was the best sound he'd ever heard. He slept in it like a stone in a river.
