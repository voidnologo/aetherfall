# Chapter 4

Sera had been listening to the valley for an hour before the engines came, and the valley had not said the same thing twice.

She sat with her back to a rock on the west ridge, high above the road. Below her and to the left was the timber stack, forty great trunks chained against the slope like a held breath. Mira was somewhere on the ridge behind her, fifty yards off, with the field glasses and the flare. Sera couldn't see her and didn't try. She kept her eyes half-closed and her hands open on her knees and let the Aether come and go across her skin.

It came in pulses. She had learned that last night, sitting here in the dusk while the others slept. The air at the Wild's edge wasn't a river, like the Quarter, or a sea, like the Wilds proper. It was weather. It gathered and broke. One moment the valley was thin and grey and quiet, smelling of nothing but leaf mould and river water. The next, something swelled up out of the ground and through the trees and over her like a warm wave. Her teeth tingled. The backs of her eyes went hot. For a few breaths the leaves on the beech below her seemed outlined in very fine silver wire. Then it ebbed, as it always did. She could find no rhythm in it. She'd tried to count for an hour. It wouldn't be counted.

So she'd stopped. Aldric would have kept trying; Aldric would have had a table by now. Sometime after midnight Sera had decided she wasn't going to know what was coming. She would know what was *here*, now, and now, and now, and she would take whatever was here and use it.

It was a strange, loose, frightening way to stand on a hillside. It was also, she thought, the only honest way.

The engines came down out of the south. She opened her eyes.

---

Kael's shot was the signal.

She heard a single flat crack from the east ridge, echoing back off the slope behind her. The whole valley seemed to draw in its breath. Then the screaming started down by the river. She made herself wait. Two seconds, Mira had said. Let them stop. Let them bunch. One. Two.

She reached.

The Aether was thin. Of course it was. A pulse had just broken and gone. The air around her was grey and empty. When she pulled, a trickle came, a cupful scooped from a dry well. She pushed it at the wedges under the timber anyway. The chains creaked. One wedge shifted. Two of the top trunks rolled half a turn and stopped, as if they'd thought about it and decided against.

*No.* She bit down on the word. *No, no...*

Below her the convoy was braking and shouting. Its back end was still rolling down the hill towards the gap. In ten seconds the last car would be through and there would be no back door to close.

She reached again, harder. This time she didn't push. She shaped a fist.

The Aether came up to meet her.

The ground might have been waiting. A pulse broke under her at the exact instant she pulled, and suddenly there was everything: the warm wave, the silver wire on every leaf, a sound like tearing cloth that only she could hear. It poured into the shape she'd made, filled it and overfilled it. The fist went out of her and hit the timber stack.

The chains snapped one after another, *ping ping ping*, harp strings cut with shears. The stack bulged. Then the whole slope below her came apart and went down the hill.

It was the loudest thing she had ever heard, louder than the mill. Wood and stone and earth roared down onto the road in a brown wave with logs tossed in it like matches. Dust went up in a wall. When it cleared, the road behind the convoy was buried under a hill of wreckage. The last car in the column sat with its back wheel against a trunk like a dog nosing a stick.

She'd done it. The door was shut.

She sat back against her rock and found she was shaking.

Two reaches in five seconds: one that was nothing, one that was everything. She could feel what the second had cost. It was in her hands, which wouldn't stop trembling, and behind her eyes, where something had gone hot and stayed hot. Under her breastbone a long ache had opened, the one she'd come to know in the tunnels: a well drawn on hard that would take time to fill. In one breath she had spent what she'd meant to spend in five minutes.

*Steady.* It was Aldric's voice in her head, the one he used when he was frightened and trying not to show it. *Steady, now.*

She took a breath, let it out, and moved.

---

Her second position was a split in a boulder thirty yards along the ridge, with a clean view down to the valley floor. She crawled to it through the bracken with her coat catching on everything, and settled into the gap with her cheek against cold stone.

Down there the world had come apart into pieces.

The lorry in the ford had stalled, with the water piling against it. On the far bank... oh. She saw Aldric's wall for the first time, and for a moment she forgot everything else. It stood across the road where it climbed out of the river, tall and wide and shimmering, the trees behind it gone soft and liquid. Even from here she could *feel* it. It had a sound, a held note on a cello, and a texture like glass with a grain in it. It was the most beautiful work she had ever seen another person do. Looking at it, she understood that Aldric was paying for every second it stood.

The convoy was trapped between the wall, the river and her hill. Men were pouring out of the grey transports with rifles and firing blind into the trees on both ridges, hers and Kael's. Bullets came through the leaves above her with a sound like ripping paper. One hit the rock beside her face and whined off it, leaving a bright grey smear on the stone. She pressed herself lower.

Then she saw Kael.

He came out of the trees at the foot of the east ridge at a dead run. She watched him cross fifty yards of gravel and grass, low and fast and weaving. Every step, she was sure, would be his last. Something kicked up the gravel behind him. Then he was across and behind a lorry, out of her sight. She let her breath go.

She followed him down the line of vehicles. Most of the time he was hidden on the river side, behind the bulk of the lorries. But she could see where he'd been. Each time he moved on, a little thread of hissing sparks came out from under a tarpaulin or a chassis. The fuses.

He came round the back of the second lorry, and there was a man in grey. Sera saw it from above, as if looking down on a chessboard. The man had a rifle. Kael had a knife. Kael was faster. The man folded up around him and fell.

Behind them both, a second man came round the corner of the transport with a pistol out, turning, raising it...

There was no time to think. She reached.

A pulse had come in while she wasn't watching, and she caught its crest. The push went out of her. The man with the pistol came off his feet as if a horse had kicked him. He hit the grey side of the transport hard enough that she heard it on the ridge, slid down it and didn't get up.

Kael touched two fingers to his forehead without looking up. Then he was gone again round the far side of the lorry.

Sera put her hands flat on the rock to stop them shaking. It didn't work.

That was three. Every time she reached, the well went lower. Now she could feel its bottom, as you feel the bed of a pond with your toes before you see it.

---

The men below had worked out where she was.

The shouting changed. A word went up and down the column, *caster, caster, west ridge*. The squat gun on top of the second transport turned on its ring and began to hammer the hillside. It started on the place where the timber had come from, forty yards along, blowing bark and splinters off the trees in sprays. Then it walked along the slope towards her, chewing.

She left her rock and crawled further along the ridge on her belly. Her elbows were bleeding. She didn't notice until later.

From the new place she could see the far end of the convoy, and the big lorry with the strange shapes under its canvas. A thread of sparks was coming out from under it. Between the lorries she glimpsed Kael kneeling at the back of the gun transport, head bent, shoulders working, setting the last charge. Fourteen yards off, a grey-moustached man was coming round the front of the same transport, unhurried, a long pistol held out at arm's length.

She saw it all with terrible clarity. Kael finished and turned. His revolver came up, smooth and fast, as she had seen it come up a hundred times in practice yards and alleys.

Nothing happened.

There was no flash and no crack. The gun stayed pointed and silent. For a heartbeat Kael stood with his arm out, looking at it as if someone had handed him a stranger's hand.

She was already reaching. But it was too far. Her well was nearly dry, and the Aether was between pulses, grey and thin. The grey-moustached man fired. Kael went back against the transport's wheel and slid down it out of sight.

Sera heard a high, thin, wordless sound that went on and on. After a while she realised it was coming from her.

Down there something banged, with a puff of white smoke. The grey-moustached man fell sideways and lay clutching his leg. Then, between the lorries, she saw Kael again on his hands and knees, crawling away from the convoy towards the open ground and the hill. He moved so slowly. Behind him on the gravel ran a dark trail.

*Get up*, she thought at him. *Get up, get up, please get up...*

The big lorry exploded.

---

It didn't sound like an explosion. It sounded like a scream, a high tearing shriek that went up from under the canvas, through the soles of her feet and into her teeth. With it came a flash that was not fire-coloured. It was the colour of the dead zones, a grey-violet, the colour of nothing. For one instant the whole valley went *flat*. The Aether didn't ebb; it was simply gone, sucked out of the air like water down a plughole. Sera felt it go like falling through a floor she'd thought was solid.

Then the second lorry went. That one was just fire, a great rolling orange fist of it, and the shockwave came up the hill and slapped her flat against the rock.

When she looked again the valley was smoke. The Aether was seeping slowly back in round the edges of where the dead light had been. Three lorries were burning and men were running. On the gravel strip below the east ridge, twenty yards from where he'd been, Kael lay on his back against a spur of grey rock.

He didn't move.

Three men in grey were walking towards him out of the smoke with their rifles up.

They didn't run. They walked, as you walk towards something that isn't going to get up. The first was already working the bolt of his rifle. They were going to stand over him and make sure.

Sera stopped thinking.

---

Later she would try to remember what she did, and fail. It was not a spell. It had no shape. She built no fist or shove or wall, and she chose nothing. If she reached for the Aether at all, it was as a drowning woman reaches for the surface: with everything, with her whole body, without a thought for what she'd find.

What she found was the pulse.

It was rolling back into the valley after the dead light, the biggest she'd felt all morning, a great slow wave. She opened herself to it like a door kicked off its hinges, and all of it came through her.

It didn't feel like casting. It felt like being a riverbed in a flood.

There was light. She saw it afterwards in Mira's face, so it must have been real: a blue-white glare coming off her skin and her hair and the rock around her, bright enough to throw shadows in full daylight. The ridge shook under her. Dust went up from the slope. Small stones began to roll downhill on their own, then larger ones. The air in front of her went tight and strange and *full*, the moment before a thunderclap. Then it went out of her.

Down in the valley, the three men walking towards Kael were picked up off the ground and thrown like leaves in a gale. They went backwards across the gravel, twenty yards, thirty, into the river. The river closed over them. The burning lorry nearest Kael rocked up on two wheels and slammed down a yard to the side. The smoke around him blew away in a ring. For thirty feet in every direction, everything not nailed down had been moved out of the way. In the middle of the bare circle Kael lay on his back by his rock, alone.

The backlash hit her a heartbeat later.

It began behind her eyes with a white-hot pressure, as though two thumbs were pushing them out from inside. Her vision went blue at the edges and stayed blue. She could feel her eyes glowing as heat on her own cheeks. When she put up her hand, their light shone pink and red through her fingers in broad daylight.

Then the floor fell out of her.

She was on her knees and didn't remember kneeling. Her hands were on the rock, shaking so hard she could hear her nails scrape the stone. The ache under her breastbone had become a hole. Everything she'd had this morning had gone out of her into that valley, and some of what she hadn't had too. The space it left was cold and enormous and roared like a seashell. A high steady ringing filled her ears, the same as in the tunnels after the barrier, but much louder.

*What did I do?* she thought, very slowly. *What was that?*

She didn't know. It hadn't been anything. It had just been everything.

---

Down in the valley, people were moving towards Kael again.

These weren't the grey men. Three researchers in civilian coats had come out from behind the little cars at the back of the column now the shooting had thinned. One had a pistol. One, of all things, still clutched a clipboard to his chest. They looked around at the burning lorries, the bodies, the wall on the far bank. One pointed at Kael and shouted to the others. They were gathering, deciding what to do.

On the east ridge a tall thin figure had come out of the rocks and was standing very still, staring down at the valley floor. She would never know how she saw him through the smoke and the blue haze at the edges of her eyes.

*Aldric.* He was holding his wall and looking at Kael, with the three men between them.

She had nothing left. She knew it. She reached anyway.

It was like lifting a bucket from a dry well with a broken rope. She scraped at the bottom of herself and found a teaspoon, a few drops. She shaped it as small as she could and sent it down the hill at the researchers' feet. She couldn't have hurt them. She only meant to *move* them.

The gravel under the three men shifted a little. One sat down hard. The other two staggered and caught each other. The one with the pistol dropped it. They looked at the ground, at each other, then up at the west ridge. Whatever they saw there, they didn't like it. They ran for the cars.

It was just enough.

Sera filled her lungs with air that tasted of smoke and copper and the blood from her own nose, and screamed across the valley with everything she had left.

"*GO!*"

On the east ridge, the tall figure was already moving.

She saw the wall on the far bank shiver. It flickered once, a candle in a draught, and then it went out. It didn't fall. It simply stopped, and the far slope was suddenly hard and clear and ordinary where it had swum a moment before. Aldric was coming down the east ridge at a run. He didn't take the deer trail. He went straight down the slope through the bracken, slipping, falling, getting up, his coat flying out behind him. She had never seen him run like that. She hadn't known he could.

He had let it go. All that work, and he'd let it go.

Sera sat back on her heels. The world pulsed, bright and dim. Her vision had narrowed to a long blue tunnel with the valley at the end of it. The tunnel was getting longer.

There was a hand on her shoulder.

She hadn't heard Mira come. But Mira was kneeling beside her in the bracken, the field glasses round her neck and the flare pistol in her belt, unfired. One hand was on Sera's shoulder. The other had found her wrist, two fingers pressed to the inside, feeling for the pulse.

"Sera." Mira's voice was very calm, the calmest Sera had ever heard it, and she knew what that meant. "Stay with me. Breathe. Are you hit? Are you hurt?"

"Tired," Sera said. It was the only word she could find, and it wasn't the right one. There wasn't a right one.

"Okay." Mira let out a breath. "Okay." She got an arm under Sera's shoulders and held her up. "Aldric's got him. You hear? Aldric's got him. Stay with me."

Sera leaned against her and watched.

Down in the valley, through the smoke, a small dark figure in a long coat burst out of the trees at the foot of the east ridge. He ran across the open gravel towards the place where Kael lay alone in his bare circle, under the burning sky.
