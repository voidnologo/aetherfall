# Story 01 — Plan

## Working Title: The Ashwick Job

## Overview

The prequel. Ash & Veil Recovery has been chartered for one week and has completed zero jobs. Inspector Renna Laine of the Greycoat Authority hires them to find Fen Harker, a Gradient Scholar measuring zone drift on the Ashwick border, who stopped checking in three days ago. The trail runs from the Wet Ember to Harker's boarding house to the decommissioned Ashworth resonance foundry, and down beneath it into old drainage tunnels where the Aether has been bleeding through for months. The Lamplighter Syndicate is moving crates of charged Aetheric artifacts through those tunnels, and is holding Harker until the last shipment goes out tonight.

This is the Quickstart adventure told as fiction. It follows the module's three acts (the lead, the foundry, the tunnels) and the module's choices as the four leads would make them. The reader who finishes it can turn to the Quickstart and play the same night.

## Purpose

- **The front door to the series.** Story 01 introduces the city, the gradient, the four leads and how they work together, with no prior knowledge needed.
- **The front door to the game.** The story ends with a link to the Quickstart: "This story is the Quickstart adventure. Play it yourself."
- **Seeds for Stories 02–04,** already relied on by them:
  - The anomalous readings beneath the foundry; the tunnels going deeper than anyone has mapped (Story 03)
  - The Syndicate operation torn apart; the Syndicate knows the Society's faces; Corrigan Fels's grudge (Story 03)
  - The west-side storm drain behind the debris, used again in Story 03
  - The marching order the team learned in these tunnels (Story 03)
  - Aldric's "Ashwick Job notebook", with the chalk code partly decoded (Story 03)
  - What Mira tells Laine about the tunnels doesn't match what they found (Story 03, Fels's leverage). The first instance of Mira's filtering
  - Kael's revolver going slow in the tunnels for the first time, barely noticed (paid off in Stories 03–04)
  - Sera's faint blue-white ring, not yet permanent

## Thematic Focus: The Gradient as Lived Experience

Three zones in one night. The neutral tavern where nothing hums. The Galvanic foundry, where guns are crisp, Sera's casting is like breathing through wet cloth, and Mira's arc pistol is at its best. The Aetheric tunnels, where the magic sings, the guns grow strange, and the arc pistol's charge needle stutters. The reader should finish the story understanding, in the body, why the zone you stand in decides what you can do.

## Tone

Noir investigation that escalates into action, then wonder. Lighter than the later stories: the Society is new and a little giddy, and nobody dies. But there's a real fight and real blood, and the last scene leaves a debt on the table.

## Structure

6 chapters, rotating tight third-person POV: Mira → Kael → Aldric → Sera → Kael → Mira. Target 11,000–13,000 words.

## Chapter Plans

### Chapter 1 — Mira: The Lead
The Wet Ember, one week chartered. Laine sits down uninvited: "Let's skip the part where you try to impress me." The folder: sketch, map, Harker's last note ("something is actively pulling the boundary"). Mira reads Laine (overworked, sceptical, more worried than she says; knows the Syndicate's been busy and won't volunteer it until Mira earns it). Mrs. Olt's boarding house: flour on her apron, the man in the long coat smoking by the lamppost for an hour. Harker's room: Mira picks the desk lock; Aldric finds the calibration notes (readings rising underground, in a Galvanic district); Kael finds boot prints under the window (someone searched first and missed the journal); Sera feels the room's faint wrongness from the instruments' case. The journal's last entry: the foundry's sub-basement, readings strongest below. Across the street, a man in a long coat, by the lamppost. Ends: he flicks his cigarette into the gutter and walks away, unhurried.

### Chapter 2 — Kael: The Foundry
Afternoon. The foundry hunches against the sky "like something that died standing up". New bright chain on old doors. Chalk under the Greycoat sign: NOT EMPTY. Two "demolition workers" eating lunch. Mira tries the inspection story and almost has it, until Aldric's eyes go to the stairwell. The fight on the loading bay, Kael's first with the Society: declaration and timing as felt by a constable (who moves first, who commits last, the knife faster than the gun). Sera's push sputters in the hum. Mira's arc pistol cracks blue-white and works perfectly here. Kael shoots the second guard in the arm and watches him get slower, clumsier, more frightened with each move: the wound taking him apart. Aldric binds it. Interrogation: Syndicate, crates that make your teeth itch, the Scholar held "till the shipment moves tonight", three more below with a woman called Corva, tunnels under the border. Ends at the head of the stairwell, the air coming up warm and copper.

### Chapter 3 — Aldric: Down
The stairwell, and the change of zones in his body: the casting headache he's had since the factory quarter lifting, the spellbook going light. Mira's charge needle flickering. The tunnels: brick drainage, condensation glowing blue, crystals like fingers. Aldric casts a scouting working (seeing ahead) and finds the chamber: three people loading crates, a bound man against the wall. He documents everything: the chalk symbols at junctions (he begins decoding them), the crystal clusters, the readings in Harker's notes made real. The marching order forms without anyone deciding: Kael first, Mira second, Sera third, Aldric last. He notices Kael tap his revolver twice, frowning. A side passage leads down, deeper than the map, colder. He writes it down. Ends: the voices ahead stop.

### Chapter 4 — Sera: Where It Sings
The Aether here is thick and close and wants to be shaped. Sera, who has spent the afternoon half-strangled by the foundry's hum, feels like a swimmer surfacing. The collapsed left passage: she moves the rubble with a careful push that comes much bigger than she asked for. Bricks fly; the noise echoes. They've lost surprise, but they have a flank. The faint ring in her eyes stings. Ends at the chamber's edge: crates, crystals, Harker with broken spectacles, and a woman in a coat too good for a tunnel who doesn't reach for her gun. "Well. That was faster than I expected."

### Chapter 5 — Kael: The Standoff
Corva Sable negotiates. Mira negotiates back. Corva's offer: take Harker, walk away, the cargo disappears tonight, a hundred marks for their trouble. Mira pushes for the manifest. A worker with a shotgun panics when Sera's eyes flare. The shotgun goes off into the vault. Chaos: Sera's force scatters the crates, glass and crystal everywhere; Kael's revolver goes slow and strange for half a heartbeat, then fires (the first misgiving, filed and forgotten); Mira's arc pistol fizzles, she draws the derringer; a pellet opens Kael's cheek. Corva goes for the storm drain on the west side and is gone. The workers surrender. Aldric's first field mending on Kael's face: the heat, the itch, the skin closing. Harker freed: "Do you know what those are?" They leave by the storm drain behind the debris, carrying three crates of evidence.

### Chapter 6 — Mira: The Ledger
Laine's office. Mira gives the account, and edits it as she goes, without quite deciding to: the crates, the Syndicate, Corva, the Scholar alive. She leaves out the passage going down, the way the crystals grew in rhythm, what Aldric's readings showed. Kael notices but doesn't say so (not yet). Laine pays 200 marks, stamps the charter: "Congratulations. You're no longer the new Society." The Wet Ember again: Harker at the bar with a new journal, "they've been mining the Aether" (half right). Sera's compass, bought that morning at a market stall, spinning on the table and settling. A man in a good coat at the end of the bar, a clove cigarette, a glass raised to them: Corrigan Fels. He doesn't sit down. "You cost me a route tonight. No hard feelings. It's a small city. I'm sure we'll do business." He pays for their drinks and leaves. Mira writes the first line in the Ledger. Ends: the four of them at the corner table; outside, the hum and the copper sweetness, and under the city, something growing.
