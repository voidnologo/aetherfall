# Chapter 4

Up in the foundry, Sera had been deaf.

Afterwards she couldn't put it any other way. All her life the Aether had been there like sound: its hum and its warmth, the copper taste on warm days in the Quarter, a presence just past the edge of sight like somebody standing behind a curtain. She'd never known a world without it. In the foundry yard, with the Galvanic hum crawling up through the cobbles into her teeth, it was simply *gone*. It felt as if someone had stuffed her ears with wax, wrapped her head in a wet blanket and told her to sing.

She'd reached for the big man with the revolver and the reach had dribbled out of her into nothing. She'd been so angry she could have cried.

Then they'd come down the stair. Somewhere around the thirtieth step the wax came out of her ears all at once, and the world *roared*.

Down here it was everywhere: in the walls, in the water running down the walls, in the little crystals growing out of the mortar. The crystals weren't just pretty. She could feel each one, like a tiny bell rung very softly, all of them in time. The air was thick with it. It pressed against her skin like warm water and got into her mouth when she breathed. It *wanted*; there was no other word. Whenever she so much as thought about reaching, it leaned towards her like a cat pushing into a hand, eager to pour.

It was the most wonderful thing she had ever felt. It frightened her half to death.

---

The voices in the vault had stopped.

The four of them stood in the blue half-light without moving. Up ahead, round the bend, there was nothing: no scrape of crates, no talk, only the drip of water and the slow breathing of the crystals.

"They know," Kael breathed. "Someone's missed a check-in. The two upstairs."

"Or they heard Aldric's nose bleeding," Mira murmured. Aldric made a small affronted sound.

Mira was thinking. Sera could see it in the stillness of her face. Mira went somewhere inside at moments like this, to a room with a map in it.

"If we go in the front," Mira said, "we go down a corridor with nowhere to stand, into a room where they're waiting for us. I don't like that." She turned to Aldric. "The fall of rubble you saw. The side passage. Where does it come out?"

Aldric closed his eyes to remember. "Into the vault. On the left. Behind the crates, I think."

"Then that's our door." Mira looked back to the first fork, where the left-hand tunnel had sloped away downward. "If it joins up."

It joined up. The left tunnel ran down and round. The crystals grew thicker as it went, until the walls were furred with them and the light was nearly bright enough to read by. Then it stopped. A wall of fallen brick, earth and broken crystal blocked it from floor to ceiling. Through the gaps at the top Sera could see the bigger blue-white glow of the vault a few yards beyond. Very faintly, she heard someone moving.

"Well," said Kael, looking at the rubble. "We'll be an hour shifting that by hand. They'll hear every brick."

Everyone looked at Sera.

---

She knew how it was supposed to go. She'd done it a hundred times. You reached into the Aether and took a little, just what you needed. You gave it a shape and a direction, and you let it go. It was small and neat, like lifting a cup off a table with your mind. In the Quarter she could move a stack of bricks this way one at a time, set them down quietly in a pile, and nobody would hear a thing.

She put out her hand towards the rubble.

She took a breath.

She reached for a cupful.

The Aether came up out of the walls and the floor and the crystals and the air itself to meet her. It didn't give her a cupful. It gave her the sea.

It went through her so fast she had no time to shape it, and there was so much she couldn't have held it if she'd tried. Behind her eyes something went white and hot. She heard a sound like tearing cloth that came from nowhere and everywhere. What left her was not a hand lifting a brick. It was a battering ram.

The rubble didn't shift. It *left*.

The whole wall went down the tunnel in one roaring rush, bricks and earth and crystal tumbling end over end. It burst through the far side into the vault like a cannon going off in a church. The noise went on and on, echoing off the vaulted ceiling, as bricks clattered and rolled across the stone floor and crystal shattered in tinkling sprays.

Then it was quiet. Dust drifted in the blue light.

Sera stood with her hand still out and her mouth open.

"I asked for a little," she said in a small voice. "I only asked for a little."

Her eyes stung. She put her fingers to them and they were hot. When she took them away there was a faint blue-white light on her fingertips, the same light as the crystals. Mira was staring at her. Sera wondered what her face looked like, and decided she didn't want to know.

Kael cleared his throat. "They know we're here now."

"They knew already," said Mira. She'd recovered first, as she always did. Her hand went to her arc pistol and away again as she remembered the needle. "Now they know we came in the side door with a bang. That's worth something. Come on. Quickly, before they've thought about it."

---

They climbed through the hole Sera had made and came out into the vault behind a stack of crates the flying bricks had shoved a foot sideways.

The room was beautiful. Before she saw the people or the danger, Sera saw the room. Nothing else existed. It was round and high, its old brick ceiling arching up into shadow, and every inch of every surface was grown over with crystal. These weren't the finger-clusters of the tunnels. They were great branching sprays, as long as her arm and in places as tall as a man, growing out of the walls and hanging from the vault like icicles. All of them glowed the same deep, pulsing blue-white, and all of them breathed together. The light moved over everything like light at the bottom of a swimming bath. It was like standing inside a heart.

Then she saw the people.

Two big men in shirtsleeves, the same sort as the pair in the yard, had taken cover behind the handcart on the far side of the room. They were pointing guns at the hole in the wall, one a revolver and one a short, ugly shotgun. They looked badly shaken. One had brick dust all down his front.

A thin man sat against the far wall with his back to the crystal and his tied hands in his lap. One lens of his spectacles was cracked across. He was staring at the four of them with his mouth slightly open, and Sera thought he looked, of all things, delighted.

In the middle of the room, beside the stack of crates, a woman stood perfectly still with her hands in the pockets of a long dark coat far too good for a tunnel.

She was perhaps forty, with dark hair pinned up neatly and a narrow, clever face. She hadn't taken cover. Sera could see the shape of a weapon under the coat, but the woman's hands stayed nowhere near it. Brick dust settled on her shoulders while she looked at each of them in turn: Kael, Mira, Aldric, and last and longest at Sera's eyes. With her head tilted a little, she might have been studying a hand of cards someone had just laid in front of her.

Then she sighed.

"Well," she said. "That was faster than I expected." She took one hand out of her pocket, empty, and held it up, palm out. "And louder. Shall we talk about how this ends without anyone getting hurt? I'd so much rather. I've had a long week."
