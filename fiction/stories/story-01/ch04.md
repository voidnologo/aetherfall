# Chapter 4

Up in the foundry, Sera had been deaf.

That was the only way she could put it, afterwards. All her life the Aether had been there the way sound is there — the hum of it, the warmth, the copper taste on warm days in the Quarter, the presence just past the edge of sight like somebody standing behind a curtain. She'd never known a world without it. And in the foundry yard, with the Galvanic hum crawling up through the cobbles into her teeth, it had simply gone. Not quiet. *Gone.* Like someone had stuffed her ears with wax and wrapped her head in a wet blanket and told her to sing.

She'd reached for the big man with the revolver and the reach had just — dribbled out of her. Into nothing. She'd been so angry she could have cried.

And then they'd come down the stair, and somewhere around the thirtieth step the wax had come out of her ears all at once, and the world had *roared*.

Down here it was everywhere. In the walls. In the water running down the walls. In the little crystals growing out of the mortar, which weren't just pretty — she could feel them, each one, like tiny bells that someone was ringing very softly, all together, in time. The air was thick with it. It pressed against her skin like warm water. It got into her mouth when she breathed. It wanted — that was the only word — it *wanted*. It leaned towards her the way a cat leans into a hand. Every time she so much as thought about reaching, she felt it gather itself, eager, ready to pour.

It was the most wonderful thing she had ever felt. It frightened her half to death.

---

The voices in the vault had stopped.

They stood in the tunnel in the blue half-light, the four of them, very still. Up ahead, round the bend, nothing. No scrape of crates. No talk. Just the drip of water and the slow breathing of the crystals.

"They know," Kael breathed. "Someone's missed a check-in. The two upstairs."

"Or they heard Aldric's nose bleeding," Mira murmured, and Aldric made a small affronted sound.

Mira was thinking. Sera could see her doing it — the stillness that came over her face, as if all of her had gone inside to a room with a map in it.

"If we go in the front," Mira said, "we go in down a corridor with nowhere to stand, into a room where they're waiting for us. I don't like that." She turned to Aldric. "The fall of rubble you saw. The side passage. Where does it come out?"

Aldric closed his eyes, remembering. "Into the vault. On the left. Behind the crates, I think."

"Then that's our door." Mira looked back the way they'd come, to the first fork, where the left-hand tunnel had sloped away downward. "If it joins up."

It joined up. The left tunnel ran down and round, and the crystals grew thicker as it went, until the walls were furred with them and the light was almost bright enough to read by. Then it stopped. A wall of fallen brick and earth and broken crystal blocked it from floor to ceiling, and through the gaps at the top, a few yards beyond, Sera could see the bigger blue-white glow of the vault, and hear — very faint — someone moving.

"Well," said Kael, looking at the rubble. "We'll be an hour shifting that by hand. And they'll hear every brick."

Everyone looked at Sera.

---

She knew how it was supposed to go. She'd done it a hundred times. You reached into the Aether — you took a little of it, just what you needed — and you gave it a shape, a push, a direction, and you let it go. Small. Neat. Like lifting a cup off a table with your mind. In the Quarter she could move a stack of bricks like this, one at a time, and set them down quietly in a pile, and nobody would hear a thing.

She put out her hand towards the rubble.

She took a breath.

She reached for a cupful.

The Aether came up out of the walls and the floor and the crystals and the air itself to meet her, and it didn't give her a cupful. It gave her the sea.

It went through her like a gust through an open window — so fast she didn't have time to shape it, so much she couldn't have held it if she'd tried. Behind her eyes something went white and hot. She heard, very clearly, a sound like tearing cloth that came from nowhere and everywhere. And the push went out of her not as a hand lifting a brick but as a battering ram.

The rubble didn't move. It *left*.

The whole wall of it went down the tunnel ahead of them in one roaring rush, bricks and earth and broken crystal tumbling end over end, and burst out through the far side into the vault like a cannon going off in a church. The noise was enormous. It went on and on, echoing off the vaulted ceiling, bricks clattering and rolling across the stone floor, crystal shattering in great tinkling sprays.

Then silence. Dust drifting in the blue light.

Sera stood with her hand still out and her mouth open.

"I asked for a little," she said, in a small voice. "I only asked for a little."

Her eyes stung. She put her fingers to them and they were hot, and when she took them away there was a faint light on her fingertips, pale and blue-white, like the light from the crystals. Mira was staring at her. Sera wondered what her face looked like, and decided she didn't want to know.

"Well," said Kael, after a long moment. "They know we're here now."

"They knew already," said Mira. She'd recovered first, as she always did. Her hand had gone to her arc pistol, and away from it again, remembering the needle. "Now they know we came in the side door with a bang. That's not nothing. Come on. Quickly. Before they've thought about it."

---

They came out through the hole Sera had made, into the vault, behind a stack of crates that had been shoved a foot sideways by the flying bricks.

It was beautiful.

That was the first thing. Before the people, before the danger — Sera saw the room, and for a moment there was nothing else. It was round, and high, and the old brick of its ceiling arched up into shadow, and every inch of every surface was grown over with crystal. Not little finger-clusters, like the tunnels. Great branching sprays of it, as long as her arm, as tall as a man in places, growing out of the walls and hanging from the vault like icicles, all of them glowing the same deep pulsing blue-white, all of them breathing together. The light moved over everything like light at the bottom of a swimming bath. It was like standing inside a heart.

The second thing was the people.

Two men in shirtsleeves, big, the same sort as the two in the yard, had taken cover behind the handcart on the far side of the room and were pointing guns at the hole in the wall — one a revolver, one a short, ugly shotgun. They looked badly shaken. One had brick dust all down his front.

Against the far wall, a thin man sat with his back to the crystal and his tied hands in his lap. His spectacles were broken — one lens cracked across. He was staring at the four of them with his mouth slightly open, and Sera thought he looked, of all things, delighted.

And in the middle of the room, standing perfectly still beside the stack of crates, with her hands in the pockets of a long dark coat that was far too good for a tunnel, was a woman.

She was perhaps forty. Dark hair pinned up neatly. A narrow, clever face. She hadn't taken cover. She hadn't drawn a weapon; Sera could see the shape of one under the coat, and the woman's hands stayed nowhere near it. She simply stood there in the blue light, with brick dust settling on her shoulders, and looked at them one after another — Kael, Mira, Aldric, and last and longest at Sera, at Sera's eyes — with her head a little on one side, the way you might look at a hand of cards someone has just laid down in front of you.

Then she sighed.

"Well," she said. "That was faster than I expected." She took one hand out of her pocket, empty, and held it up, palm out. "And louder. Shall we talk about how this ends without anyone getting hurt? I'd so much rather. I've had a long week."
